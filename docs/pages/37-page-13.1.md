# 13. SHIP MOVEMENT

## 13.1 HYPER DRIVES

Hyper drives (HDRV) are used to propel ships through hyper space, either within a solar system or from one system to another.
The engine's jump range is one light year times its TL.
A ship may have as many HDRV as desired, but all HDRV on one ship should be at the same TL.
(If different TL's are used, the computer will use the lowest TL in computing the jump range).
Each HDRV can propel 1,045 mass units times its TL.
If the mass of the ship exceeds this, the ship will not respond to move (MOV) or jump (JMP) orders.
In calculating the number of HDRV necessary to propel the ship, the mass of the HDRV's are always counted.
Ships with less than 100 HDRV, regardless of the size of the ship, will not respond to MOV or JMP orders.
Also, if a ship does not have enough fuel to complete a MOV or JMP order, the ship will not respond to that order.
(In other words, a partial MOV or JMP will not be made).
Only the HDRV required by the mass of the ship will use fuel.
A minimum of 100 HDRV are always used.

## 13.2 SPACE DRIVES

A ship must have at least one space drive in order to establish itself in a stable orbit around a planet.
A ship without a space drive establishes itself in a decaying orbit which has a 50% chance of terminating each turn.
Other than the one space drive used to maintain orbit, space drives are not used for ship movement, per se.
They are used in combat, however, See 15.9.2 for details.
(Fuel is used by space drives only during combat).

## 13.3 MOVEMENT

Ships never land.
Transfers of POP and other units are done by TPT's.
There are two ways in which a ship may be propelled.
It can move (with a MOV order), or it can jump (with a JMP order).
Ships can only go to coordinate points where there is a solar system.

### 13.3.1 JUMPS

Jumps are not made within a solar system; they are only made from one system to another.
When a JMP order is written, a ship may jump from any orbit in a system to orbit 0 in another system.
(To move from orbit 0 to another orbit, the player writes a MOV order).
Each jump requires one turn to complete.
The distances in jumps are measured in light years.
To find the distance between one system and another, a system probe is made.
(See Chapter 14 for more information).

#### 13.3.1.1 Over-jumping

If a ship is ordered to JMP more than its HDRVs' TL in light years, there is a chance that either the HDRV will melt (still completing the JMP), or the HDRV will blow up (destroying the ship), or that the ship will arrive completely intact.
