# 1. INTRODUCTION

EMPYREAN CHALLENGE is a computer moderated, hidden movement, play-by-mail science fiction game.
The goal of the game is the control of star cluster consisting of 100 solar systems.
All the systems have one to 10 planets per star but only 10 systems are inhabited at the start of the game; each of the ten systems having one inhabited world.
Each of the ten inhabited planets has 15 nations; the 15 players on a planet rule the nations individually and together constitute a race.

The game strategy relies heavily on diplomacy and economics.
Players will find that these considerations, as well as military ones, will govern their activities.

At the start of the game, each player faces the problems of overpopulation, diminishing resources, heavy competition from neighboring nations, and developing interstellar travel.
Later, the player's challenges occur off planet, usually involving other races.

Each game will have an entirely different set of locations for the 100 solar systems, so that no two games will be entirely alike.

These revised rules have been printed in a loose-leaf format; so that corrections and additions to the rules may be made easily, eliminating the need for separate clarifications sheets, etc.
Should a correction be necessary, the corrected page will be mailed out, with the corrected area indicated to save time, for players to insert in the rules.
The incorrect page would then be destroyed.
Players can either keep their rules together in the black side clip they are in, or remove the clip and insert the rules into a 3-ring binder.
The rules have been 3-hole punched for this purpose.

This game is one of the most complex and detailed computer moderated correspondence games that exist.
We suggest that novice gamers, or gamers not used to this type of game, begin playing as Regents.
Regents start the game with limited responsibility and should receive a good deal of coaching from their Rulers.

However you choose to play, good gaming and good luck.

# 2. HISTORY OF THE EMPYREAN CLUSTER

Some time ago, an Empire was born.
It prospered and grew to encompass myriad solar systems and hundreds of races.
The Empire was mighty and ponderous.
Then, as in other empires, corruption and oppression set in; stirring the subject planets' always present desire for independence to a fever pitch.

One planet, whose rulers were braver than most, did rebel and was promptly destroyed.
This sparked the revolution and started a galactic holocaust.

During the worst years of the war, when biological warfare was used and planets were being decimated right and left; a group of leaders from the rebel worlds decided to hedge their races' continued existence.
Their plan was to colonize ten worlds, all located in the distant Empyrean Cluster.
The planets would be in separate solar systems and would each be inhabited by one of ten races.
As the rebel worlds couldn't spare much in technological aid, it was decided that the colonies would be agricultural in nature, rather than industrial.

It was not long after the colonies were established that the rebel worlds, having finally overthrown the Empire, began to fight with each other for the remains of the Empire.
These struggles became so intense that hardly a planet escaped massive injury.
The result of this tremendous destruction left virtually no planet capable of interstellar flight and no single interplanetary government functioning.

The colonies, isolated now, had no problem with food supplies, but their manufacturing capabilities were very limited.
Their technologies improved, over the centuries, until fairly high levels were achieved; resulting in the re-discoveries of antigravity and hyper-space travel.

However, the colonies did not escape the great wars unscathed.
The biological warfare used on the founding rebel planets had affected the colonists' genetic structure, mutating two genes.
When an individual only carried one of these genes, he was not affected; and the gene, after several generations, tended to revert to its original form.
But, when both genes were combined, they became dominant, and he or she became telepathic in a limited way.
The telepathic ability enabled the individual to communicate mentally with anyone who had the telepathic genes and who was related by blood.

Members of the telepathic families had to marry outsiders at first, but as the families grew, they became large enough so that the members could marry within the families themselves and still bypass the dangers of inbreeding.
To marry within a family was preferred as telepathic communication between husband and wife could be had only if they were related by blood.

These Families, on the ten planets, began to acquire great wealth and power due to the inherent closeness engendered by their shared ability.
Eventually, this concentration of power in the hands of the Families, and the growth and separation of the population, caused the formation of nations; each of which was ruled by the head of a Family.

A Ruler used the rest of the Family members to fill all important government positions, as they acted as one with him and could communicate with him instantly; and when space exploration began, the Family members became his ship captains and colony governors.
As a Ruler's ships ranged further from the home planet, telepathy between planet and ship did not fail, showing that the telepathic range seemed to be unlimited.

At this point, each of the ten planets has approximately 15 Family-ruled nations; each nation competing with each other and with the other races for the control of the Empyrean Cluster.

# 3. GAME SET UP

## 3.1 COORDINATES

### 3.1.1 COMPUTER MAP

The cluster is mapped on a three-dimensional grid, and is 30 light years per side.
Each axis is numbered in light years.
Star systems are designated in the computer by their coordinates, which are stored as three two-digit numbers.
These numbers range from 0 to 30, an example being 28/02/28.

There are 100 star systems in a game.

### 3.1.2 MULTIPLE STAR SYSTEMS

If two or more stars are at the same coordinates, they are known as a binary system (two stars), or a trinary system (three stars), etc.
Each star of the system has 10 orbits around it; each star with its planets revolves around the other star (or stars).
There is an orbit 0 surrounding the entire star system and its planets, which serves all the stars of a multiple star system, and it will be explained later.
A multiple star system is designated by adding a letter to the coordinates.
Thus, star B of system of 28/02/18 designates the second star of a multiple star system.
By convention, Star A always designates the first star of a system, even if the system contains only a single star.

### 3.1.3 COORDINATES ON THE PRINTOUT

All coordinates are given relative to each race's home planet.
So, each race's home planet coordinates are always 0/0/0.
At the start of the game, a player gets a free magnitude-10 probe report on his printout.
This will give the coordinates of all stars within 10 light years from the home planet, their distances from the home planet, and a list showing the type of planet in each orbit of the home system.

## 3.2 SOLAR SYSTEMS

Each star ("solar system") has at least one planet, with no more than 10 planets per star.
A star has 11 orbits, numbered from 0 to 10; number 1 is closest to the star; and number 10 is farthest from it.
Orbits 1 - 10 may be occupied by one planet of any type per orbit ; orbit 0 is never occupied by a planet.
If there are two or more stars in a coordinate point, orbit 0 surrounds the stars and the planets, as explained above.
Ships from other systems always arrive in orbit 0.

There are from 500 to 700 planets in a game.

## 3.3 PLANETS

### 3.3.1 TYPES OF PLANETS

Each star has from one to ten planets, which are located in orbits 1 through 10.

Not every orbit will contain a planet and no orbit will contain multiple planets.

There are three possible types of planets: gas giants, terrestrial, and asteroids.

#### 3.3.1.1 Gas Giants

Gas Giants have huge atmospheres.
Surface colonies are located only on the moons of these planets.
Gas giants and their moons are treated as one body.

#### 3.3.1.2 Terrestrial

Terrestrial planets are too small to retain the huge atmospheres of gas giants, but large enough to have become spherical.
Terrestrial in this context does not necessarily mean earthlike or habitable.

#### 3.3.1.3 Asteroids

An asteroid is so small that it is able to retain its irregular shape.
The term "asteroid" in this game will refer to an entire asteroid belt, and is also considered to be a planet.
The asteroids in an orbit are treated as one body.

### 3.3.2 HABITABILITY NUMBERS

A habitability number indicates the number of population units who can live on a planet's surface, in OPEN colonies, without being overcrowded or having to import food.
Habitability numbers do not affect, and are not affected by, the operations of enclosed surface colonies.
Each planet has a habitability number ranging from 0 to 25.
Should a planet have a population exceeding the limit indicated by the habitability number, the death rate of *all* the OPEN colonies on the planet will be increased.
(Further explained in section 5.3).

### 3.3.3 RESOURCE DEPOSITS

Each planet has a maximum of 40 natural resource deposits.
Each deposit contains 1,000,000-99,000,000 units of: gold, fuel, metallics and non-metallics.

## 3.4 TIME SCALE

One turn in the game equals one quarter of a (galactic standard) year.

## 3.5 INITIAL PRINTOUT AND TURNSHEET

When a player enters a game, she receives an initial printout which lists:

* the number of factory groups she has
* the number of factories per group
* the groups' technological level
* the kind of unit each group makes

It also lists
* the type of population units she has, and their quantity
* the types of weapon units she has and their quantity
* the types of transportation she has and their quantity
* the types of resources she has and their quantity
* any miscellaneous items she has

The printout also shows her player ID#, her home colony's ID#, her race number, and a free magnitude-10 probe report as mentioned previously.

The initial turnsheet will be enclosed with the initial printout and should be sent back to us before the date indicated on the printout.
All factory groups start with work in process already running.

# 5. POPULATION UNITS

## 5.1 TYPES OF POPULATION UNITS

### 5.1.1 UNEMPLOYABLES (UEM)

Unemployables is the entire population that is not USK, PRO, or SLD.
They are not part of the work force or military; they may not be ordered to perform any task.

### 5.1.2 UNSKILLED WORKERS (USK)

Unskilled workers are those who do not require long apprenticeships or a great deal of training.
USK can be drafted to become soldiers or trainees; or can be assembled as part of construction worker units; or they may be part of the work force for farms, mines, and factories.
All disbanded soldiers become USK.

### 5.1.3 PROFESSIONALS (PRO)

Professionals require long apprenticeships or a great deal of training.
PRO's are graduated from the trainees, and are used to train the trainees, as well.
They are also used to help make up spy units and construction worker units, and are part of the work force for farms, mines, and factories.
Soldiers, (5% ANNUALLY), retire from the military and become PRO's.

### 5.1.4 SOLDIERS (SLD)

Soldiers include all military personnel.
SLD are drafted from USK, and may be used to help form spy units.
All SLD not used in spy units are used in combat.
5% of all soldiers retire annually, (on every fourth turn), and become PRO's.
Any number of soldiers may be disbanded on any turn, with a disband (DSB) order, and these become USK.

### 5.1.5 SPIES (SPYS)

Spies conduct espionage activities.

A spy cadre can be assembled, with an ASM (assemble) order, or disassembled, with a DAS (disassemble) order, on any turn.
The number of SPYS that can be assembled is limited to the number of PRO's and SLD's a player has that are loyal.
Each spy cadre is made up of 1 PRO and 1 SLD.
Each spy cadre, therefore, is made up of 2 population units.

### 5.1.6 CONSTRUCTION WORKERS (CNW)

Construction workers execute ASM (assemble), DAS (disassemble), and SCR (scrap) orders.

A construction worker cadre can be assembled, with an ASM (assemble) order, or disassembled, with a DAS (disassemble) order, on any turn.
Each CNW is made up of 1 PRO and 1 USK.
Each CNW, therefore, is made up of 2 population units.
The number of CNWs that can be assembled is limited to the number of PRO's and USK's a player has.
Also, CNWs are used to do assembly portions of STP and ADN orders.

Note: CNWs are not needed for merge, combine, or give orders.

### 5.1.7 REBELS

Rebel units are merely a tally of the number of population units in each population type or group willing to rebel.
They are found in *all* groups, except spies, and soldiers.
The portion of SLD willing to rebel are known as *rebel soldiers*; rebel soldiers determine the success or failure of a rebellion.
There are no rebels at all in the SPYS.

### 5.1.8 TRAINEES (TRN)

Trainees are USK in training to become PRO's.
Each 100 TRN requires one PRO to train them.
5% of all trainees graduate each turn.

### 5.1.9 POPULATION UNITS (POP)

Each unit of population consists of 100 people.
Printouts and turnsheets deal only with whole population units.

## 5.2 POPULATION INCREASES

### 5.2.1 UNEMPLOYABLE INCREASES

Unemployable increases are caused by births and SLD's captured in combat.
All births occur in this population type, never in any other.
All births occur in colonies, never in ships.
Birth increases occur each turn and are never less than 0.25%, or more than 2.5%, of the total population of a colony.
Births decrease as the standard of living rises and also as the total quantity of population units approaches the amount indicated by the habitability number.
The number of wounded becoming UEM is determined by the total number of combat casualties.
(30% of the total become UEM).
Increases due to births and wounded are automatic and need no orders.

### 5.2.2 UNSKILLED INCREASES

Unskilled increases are drawn automatically by the computer from the UEM, using the following ratio:

| UEM as a % of s/c's POP | % of UEM that become USK |
|-------------------------|--------------------------|
| 0% ... 19%              | 0%                       |
| 20% ... 29%             | 1%                       |
| 30% ... 39%             | 2%                       |
| 40% ... 49%             | 4%                       |
| 50% ... 59%             | 8%                       |
| 60% ... 69%             | 16%                      |
| 70% ... 100%            | 25%                      |

All disbanded SLD become USK, (when a DSB order is written).

### 5.2.3 PROFESSIONAL INCREASES

Professional increases are caused by the influx of trainees each turn, and by the entry of retiring SLD.
5% of all SLD retire each *year* (every fourth turn), and 5% of all TRN graduate each *turn*.
These percentages become PRO's automatically.

### 5.2.4 SOLDIER INCREASES

Soldier increases are due to the draft of USK.
The player writes a draft (DRF) order for the SLD needed, and that number is deducted from the quantity of USK and added to the existing SLD on that turn.

### 5.2.5 SPY INCREASES

SPYS increases are ordered by the player, using an assemble (ASM) order.
An ASM order for SPYS takes the necessary quantity of PRO's and SLD's from each group, and puts them together to form the SPYS ordered.
1 SLD and 1 PRO assembled become 1 SPYS.
A SPYS can be assembled or disassembled (ASM or DAS) on any turn.
The number of SPYS assembled is limited to the number of SLD and PRO units in a s/c that are loyal.
SPYS can't be formed from rebels.

### 5.2.6 CONSTRUCTION WORKER INCREASES

CNW increases are ordered using an assemble (ASM) order.
This order takes the necessary quantity of PRO's and USK's from each group, and puts them together to form the CNW's ordered.
1 USK and 1 PRO become 1 CNW.
A CNW can be ASM or DAS on any turn.
The number of CNW's assembled is limited to the number of PRO's and USK's a s/c has.

### 5.2.7 REBEL (AND REBEL SOLDIER) INCREASES

These increases may be based upon several conditions which may occur (individually or collectively) in a s/c because of a player's mismanagement:

1. General population is underpaid, or back pay exists.
2. General population is underfed.
3. Lack of housing space (occurs only in OPC's - people sleep in streets).
4. Starvation occurring.
   Causes greater rebel increases than #2.
5. Jettisoning people (to colony 0).

Rebels also increase if a foreign nation has his "E" spies incite loyal population units to rebel.

### 5.2.8 TRAINEE INCREASES

Trainees increase when the player writes a DRF order for a quantity of TRN to be trained as PRO's.
This number is deducted from the USK and added to the existing trainee units.
5% of all TRN graduate each *turn* as professionals.

## 5.3 POPULATION DECREASES

### 5.3.1 UNEMPLOYABLE DECREASES

Unemployable decreases occur automatically when a portion of UEMs are used to increase the number of USK.
See 5.2.2.

### 5.3.2 UNSKILLED DECREASES

Unskilled decreases are due to the DRF orders for trainees or soldiers or ASM orders for CNW.

### 5.3.3 PROFESSIONAL DECREASES

Professional decreases are due to ASM orders for SPYS and CNW's.

### 5.3.4 SOLDIER DECREASES

Soldier decreases are due to ASM orders for SPYS; DSB orders which disband, retirement (5% per *year*), and combat casualties.
Disbanded SLD become USK.

### 5.3.5 SPY DECREASES

Spy decreases are due to assassination by foreign spies, and by DAS orders that disassemble them.
Each spy unit disassembles into 1 PRO and 1 SLD.

### 5.3.6 CONSTRUCTION WORKER DECREASES

Construction worker decreases are due to DAS orders that disassemble them; each CNW disassembles into 1 USK and 1 PRO.

### 5.3.7 REBEL DECREASES

Rebel decreases are caused by the Ruler ordering his spies to convert those rebels into loyal population units (see 19. Espionage), and by improved standard of living.
Rebel soldiers are decreased in the same manner as rebels.

### 5.3.8 TRAINEE DECREASES

Trainee decreases are caused by DSB orders which disband them, (they become USK again), and by graduation as PRO's (5% per turn).

### 5.3.9 DEATH RATE

The death rate per s/c per turn is 0.625%.
The computer applies this percentage to each population type within the s/c.
This percentage is increased for all open colonies when the planet's habitability number x 10,000,000 becomes less than the total POP in all the OPC's of the planet.

# 6. BASIC NEEDS (FOOD, CONSUMER GOODS, LIFE SUPPORT)

## 6.1 FOOD

### 6.1.1 CONSUMPTION

Each population unit (POP) automatically consumes one quarter of a food unit (FOOD) per turn, unless the Ruler rations the s/c.
If the rationed amount of food is less than 1/16 of a food unit per POP per turn, the computer will divide the food equally, and check for starvation, according to the Starvation Rule below.
Ration orders can not be more than 100% (1/4 food unit per POP), or less than 25% (1/16 food unit per POP)

#### 6.1.1.1 Starvation Rule

    (M-R) ÷ M = S

Where S is the fraction of the population of the s/c that starves, M is the minimum amount of food units that can be allotted without starvation, (or 1/16 of a food unit), and R is the rationed amount of food per POP (when less than M).

##### 6.1.1.1.1 Example

Due to a food shortage, the computer rations each POP to 1/20 of a food unit, (20%).

```text
(1/16 - 1/20) ÷ 1/16 = S
(5/80 - 4/80) ÷ 5/80 = S
16/80 = 1/5 of the population starved.
```

Note: 30% OF THE STARVING POP DIE EACH **TURN**.
(Fractions rounded up).

### 6.1.2 RATION PERCENTAGE

The percentage of a ration (RTN) order is the percentage of the normal 1/4 food unit per population unit.

## 6.2 CONSUMER GOODS

### 6.2.1 CONSUMPTION

All population types (except unemployables) are paid in consumer goods (CNGD).
Unemployables are paid nothing.

All PAY orders are standing orders; they remain the same until changed by the player.
At the start of the game, all unskilled (USK) units are paid 0.125 CNGD per turn; soldiers (SLD) are paid 0.25 CNGD per turn; and professionals (PRO) are paid 0.375 per turn.
Construction workers (CNW) are paid the USK rate plus the PRO rate.
Spies (SPYS) are paid the PRO pay rate plus the SLD pay rate.
Trainees are paid at the USK rate.
To change the pay rate, the player writes a PAY order.

### 6.2.2 BACK PAY

If a s/c does not have enough CNGD to pay its people, then the computer adds the unpaid amount to a record the computer keeps which is shown on each printout, called the Back Pay Amount.
Back pay has the effect of increasing rebels.
The larger the amount of back pay in proportion to the population, the greater the increase in rebels.
The effect of back pay on ships is only one tenth the effect in colonies.
Back pay is decreased when the s/c has more CNGD than it needs to meet its payroll.

## 6.3 LIFE SUPPORT

### 6.3.1 CONSUMPTION

Life support units (LFS) are used in s/c's to recycle air and water.
They will support the number of POP equal to their TL squared.
For instance, a TL-5 LFS (LFS-5) will support 25 POP.
A LFS uses one fuel unit times its TL per turn (i.e. a LFS-5 uses 5 fuel units per turn).
Only OPC's do not require LFS.
LFS on OBC's in orbits 1-5 do not use FUEL, but use solar power.

### 6.3.2 DEATH RATE AFFECTED

If the LFS capacity of a s/c is exceeded, the death rate will be increased as follows:

    N = ((P x 10) + 1) x D

Where P is the fraction of population over the LFS limit, D is the current death rate, and N is the new death rate.

Each turn the s/c's LFS capacity is not exceeded, the death rate will be cut in half until it reaches normal (0.0625%).
Lack of LFS does not cause rebels.

# 7. SHIPS AND COLONIES

## 7.1 TYPES

There are: ships (SHP), enclosed surface colonies (ESC), open surface colonies (OPC), and orbiting colonies (OBC).

## 7.2 LOCATION RESTRICTIONS

### 7.2.1 OPEN COLONIES

OPC's are not allowed on planets with a habitability number of 0.

### 7.2.2 ENCLOSED SURFACE COLONIES

ESC's may be on any planet.

### 7.2.3 ORBITING COLONIES

OBC's may be located in any orbit around a star, whether or not there is a planet there.

### 7.2.4 SHIPS

SHP's may be located in any orbit around a star, whether or not there is a planet there, and may be moved from orbit to orbit, or from its orbit to orbit 0 of another system.
All incoming ships arrive in orbit 0 of the system.

## 7.3 STRUCTURAL REQUIREMENTS

### 7.3.1 DEFINITION

Assembled STUN, LTSU, or SLSU must encompass the VOLUME of any s/c's units and population, except NMTS and METS in OPC's and ESC's.
The total volume units times the STRUCTURE RATIO, (see 7.3.1.1), gives the s/c structural requirement.
In OPC's and ESC's, NMTS and METS are stored outside the colony.

#### 7.3.1.1 STRUCTURE RATIO

The minimum STUN, LTSU, or SLSU required to enclose a single volume unit are:

|                                 |    |
|---------------------------------|----|
| Open colonies (OPC)             | 1  |
| Enclosed surface colonies (ESC) | 5  |
| Orbiting colonies (OBC)         | 8  |
| Ships (SHP)                     | 10 |

This means that structural requirements of a SHP are determined by how much it carries.
If a SHP is to transport 100,000 volume units, it will require a minimum of 1,000,000 STUN, LTSU, or SLSU.
For the number of volume units per item see the UNIT CHARACTERISTICS SUMMARY on the back cover.

### 7.3.2 STRUCTURAL REQUIREMENT EXCEEDED

Open colonies may exceed their structural requirement.
When this happens, POP are forced into the fields outside the colony to live.
Twenty-five percent of the dispossessed POP's will turn rebel each turn.
In ships, orbiting colonies, and enclosed surface colonies, structural requirement excesses prevent new items from entering the colony.
In the case of an excess of POP, LFS would not be sufficient, and some would die.

## 7.4 SET UPS

### 7.4.1 DESCRIPTION

A set up (STP) order establishes a new s/c.
A s/c does not have to be established all in one turn; part may be set up on one turn, and the rest of it can be built on following turns, using add-on orders and transfer orders.
The computer places newly set up ships between 2 and 3 distance units from the planet.

### 7.4.2 S/C ID NUMBERS

The player does not assign an ID number to a s/c.
When the computer receives a set up order, it assigns the ID number for the s/c automatically; the player sees it on the next printout.

### 7.4.3 SET UP RESTRICTIONS

S/c's may establish any other type or number of ships and colonies, providing restrictions listed below are met.

#### 7.4.3.1 Restrictions on Type of S/C

A player may not set up a colony if this would result in more than one of each type of colony at the same location.
This does not apply to ships; only 99 ships may be set up at the same location by the same player.

#### 7.4.3.2 Restrictions Ruler - Regent

A Ruler may not set up a colony of one kind if his Regent already owns a colony of that kind at that location.
Conversely, a Regent may not set up the same kind of colony that his Ruler has in the same location, or that another of his Ruler's regents has in that location.

#### 7.4.3.3 Permission to Colonize

A player may not set up a colony on a planet controlled by another player, unless he has communicated with the other player asking his permission to colonize, and the controller has agreed and has written a permission to colonize order (PTC).
There is no limit to the number of players that may establish colonies at a location, as long as PTC is granted, and as long as the limit of one of each type of colony per location per player is observed.
When a player has received PTC on a planet, he does not need it to set up more colonies there.

#### 7.4.3.4 Failures to Set Up

All set-up orders, whether for ships or colonies, will abort if no population are included in the set up order.
A ship set up will also abort if no SDRV are included.
Portions of a STP, or all of it, may fail due to a shortage of construction workers, transports, fuel (for the transports), or due to a shortage of STUN, LTSU, or SLSU (there must be enough to hold the units being STP).
The s/c's otherwise have no size limitations, and players may build them as they choose.

#### 7.4.3.5 SET-UPS FOR OTHER PLAYERS

STP's may be done for other players only if they are in the same race as the player doing the set-up.
A s/c is STP by only one s/c; STP's are never joint ventures.

### 7.4.4 PERSONNEL

Personnel requirements will vary with the equipment being set up.
The section entitled Personnel Requirements/Unit on the Unit Characteristics Summary, shown on the back cover, gives this information.

### 7.4.5 HOW STP ORDERS WORK

When a player wishes to set up a new s/c, he writes a set-up (STP) order for an existing s/c, specifying the type of s/c it is to set up, and which units will be used in constructing it.
The computer will cause construction worker units and transports to do the transferring and assembling of the items in the set-up order.
No other order is necessary for the initial establishment, but ADN and TFR orders, on later turns, may increase the size of the s/c.
All the materials needed to create the new s/c come from the establishing s/c's POP, non-assembly items, and unassembled items.

### 7.4.6 CONSTRUCTION WORKERS AND SET-UPS

The automatic assembling by construction worker units (CNW) in the new s/c, applies only to unassembled items (UNA).
1 CNW assembles 500 mass units of UNA.
All CNW listed in the STP order do as many assemblies as they are able to perform, and remain in the new s/c when done.
All the assembling in a STP order only lasts one turn.
All CNW listed in the STP order will stay in the new s/c regardless of whether their numbers are greater or less than the number necessary to perform the assemblies in one turn.
If there are not enough CNW listed in the STP order for the required assemblies, they are helped out by the CNW in the establishing s/c.
The helping CNW return to the establishing s/c when they are done.
If there are no CNW listed in the STP order at all, all the work is done by CNW of the establishing s/c, and they all return to it.
If there are not enough CNW in the establishing s/c either, as many items in the STP order are assembled as possible, **IN THE ORDER IN WHICH THEY ARE LISTED IN THE STP ORDER**.
(A player should therefore list the STUN (or LTSU or SLSU) first, followed by the other units in the order of their importance).
UNA that can not be assembled by the CNW are simply left in the establishing s/c.

### 7.4.7 TRANSPORTS AND SET-UPS

If there are no transports (TPT's) listed in the STP order, then TPT's from the establishing s/c are used, which return to the establishing s/c when done.
All the work in a STP is accomplished in one turn.
If there are more TPT listed in the STP than necessary to do the required transfers, then those unnecessary TPT are used by the establishing s/c for other work.
All the TPT in the STP return to the new s/c.
If there are some TPT listed in the STP, but not enough to do the required transfers, they are helped by TPT's from the establishing s/c.
The helping TPT's do their transfers and return to the establishing s/c when done.
If there are not enough TPT in the establishing s/c, as many items in the STP are done as possible, **IN THE SEQUENCE IN WHICH THEY ARE LISTED IN THE STP**.
Items that can not be transferred are left in the establishing s/c.

### 7.4.8 ASSEMBLIES AND SET-UPS

Items listed in the STP, excluding population units (POP), are taken from the establishing s/c's storage.
Storage consists of non-assembly items (NON) and unassembled items (UNA).
NON are considered to be whole or complete, and do not require assembling (or dis-assembling).
UNA are items that have been disassembled to save space in storage.
POP listed in a STP are taken from the establishing s/c's population groups.
Items listed in a STP are never taken from the establishing s/c's assembled items.
NON listed in a STP go to storage at the new s/c immediately.
(This is done automatically - no order from the player is necessary).
UNA in a STP are assembled by CNW and used in the new s/c; they do not go into storage, **EXCEPT FOR MINES AND FACTORIES**.
(This is also done automatically and needs no special order from the player).
POP go into the new s/c's population groups automatically.
Mines listed in a STP are not automatically assembled upon arrival in the new s/c; they are put into storage and will need an assemble order on the next turn to start them functioning.
Factory units will be put into storage in the new s/c, unless in the STP order they were order to manufacture something, in which case they will begin on the following turn automatically.

### 7.4.9 SET-UP LOCATIONS

The s/c being set-up can be established only in the same orbit as the s/c that is doing the set-up.
If a ship or orbiting colony is being set-up, it will be in orbit.
Note: Ships NEVER land.
If an open colony or enclosed surface colony is being set-up, it will be on the surface of a planet.

## 7.5 ADD-ONS

### 7.5.1 DESCRIPTION

An add-on order (ADN) adds units onto an existing s/c, using the TPT's, CNW's, and materials from another s/c.
The s/c doing the ADN does not have to be the establishing s/c.
There is no limit to the size of an ADN.

### 7.5.2 ADN RESTRICTIONS

An existing s/c may ADN to any other type or number of ships and colonies, so long as the restrictions listed below are met.

#### 7.5.2.1 ADN Abortions

Portions of an ADN, or all of an ADN, may fail due to a shortage of CNW, fuel for the TPT's, TPT's, or STUN (or LTSU or SLSU) to hold all the additional units.

### 7.5.3 HOW ADN ORDERS WORK

A player wishing to ADN to a s/c writes an ADN order to an existing s/c, telling it to add certain items to an existing s/c.
The computer will cause construction worker units and transports to do the transferring of items in the ADN order.
All the materials needed to do the ADN come from the adding-on s/c's POP, NON, and UNA.

### 7.5.4 CONSTRUCTION WORKERS AND ADD-ONS

The rules regarding CNW's and set ups apply to add-ons, too.
See 7.4.6.

### 7.5.5 TRANSPORTS AND ADD-ONS

The rules regarding TPT's and set-ups apply to add-ons, too.
See 7.4.7.

### 7.5.6 ASSEMBLIES AND ADD-ONS

All rules regarding assemblies and set-ups apply to ADN's too, (see 7.4.8) with the addition that factories in an ADN can also be ordered to be added-on to an existing factory group.

### 7.5.7 ADD-ON LOCATIONS

The s/c doing the ADN must be at the same location (or orbit) as the s/c being added-on-to.

### 7.5.8 SPECIAL RULES

A Ruler can not ADN to s/c belonging to another Ruler's regent, only to one belonging to his own Regent.
A Regent can only ADN to s/c's belonging to his Ruler or another Regent of his Ruler.
The receiving Ruler or Regent does not have to write an order allowing the ADN.
A Ruler can ADN to a s/c belonging to himself or to a Regent (neither case requires an allow add-on order).
A Ruler not only can ADN and allow an add-on to his own s/c's and those of his Regents, he also can with other Rulers of the same race.
They must write an allow add-on order (AAN) or the order will fail.

## 7.6 JUNKING

A junk order (JNK) places all units that make up a s/c into another s/c's storage, after dis-assembling all the assembled items.
Players can JNK only their own s/c's, not other players' s/c's.
Both the s/c doing the JNK and the s/c to be JNK'ed must be at the same orbit.
CNW's and TPT's are used to perform this order.
POP's do not go into storage; they join the junking s/c's POP's.
When a s/c has been junked, it ceases to exist.

## 7.7 GIFTS

A gift order (GIV) gives away s/c's - *only* s/c's, not any other item.
Rulers can not GIV to another Ruler's regents, only his own.
A regent can GIV only to his Ruler or to another Regent of his Ruler.
Giving is allowed only between Ruler and Regent.
The receiver must write an ACP order - this applies to Regents, as well.
Finally, under no circumstances may a player give away his home colony.
He may, however, give away someone else's home colony that he has conquered.

## 7.8 MERGING

A merge order (MRG) merges two s/c's into one s/c.
The two s/c's must be of the same type, must belong to the same player, and must be in the same orbit.
The total number of factory (FCT) groups in the two s/c's must not exceed 25.
Neither CNW's nor TPT's are necessary to perform a MRG order.

## 7.9 CONTROL OF PLANETS

Each planet is controlled directly by a colony, (indirectly by a player; this means that if the controlling colony is overthrown, the control of that world will be terminated immediately).
A player, wishing to set-up a colony on a planet that is controlled by another player, must first contact the controller via diplomatic message, asking his permission to colonize the planet.
If the controller agrees, he issues a permission to colonize (PTC) order.
If the planet is not controlled, the player may set up a colony there with a STP order, and then on the following turn write an order for control of the planet (CON).
The colony set-up may be an orbiting colony, an enclosed surface colony, or an open colony.
A player may give up control of a planet by writing an uncontrol (UNC) order.
A player with a colony on a world where control has been given up may take control by writing a CON order.
Once a player has received permission to colonize from the controller of the planet, he may set up others colonies on the planet without further permission to colonize orders.
**HOME PLANETS MAY NOT BE CONTROLLED.**
Controlling planets is the object of the game (see chapter 22, Victory Conditions).
The player controlling a planet may name it.
Any planet controlled by a Regent counts toward his Ruler's total.
If all the s/c's on a home planet are controlled by races other than the home planet's original race, the planet is no longer considered a home planet, and all rules concerning control apply to it.

# 8. PRODUCTION

## 8.1 FARMING

Farming is done by farm (FRM) units.
All the farm units at each technological level (TL) form a FRM group.
There can be only one group *per TL* in a s/c.
For instance one s/c could have one group of 50,000 FRM-1, and 30,000 FRM-2 and 90,000 FRM-3.
In order for the farm units to be taken from storage and put to work, the player assembles them into a FRM group.
This is done with an ASM (assembly) order, and is explained in detail in Chapter 9, Assembly.

### 8.1.1 AMOUNT OF FOOD PRODUCED

The amount of food produced per turn per farm unit depends on the unit's TL.
A FRM-1 unit (TL-1 farm unit) will produce 25 food units per turn.
A FRM-2 through FRM-10 will produce 5 times the TL in food units per turn.
So, a FRM-2 will produce 10 food units per turn.

### 8.1.2 EFFECT OF SHORTAGES

A shortage of fuel or labor will cause the FRM's to not produce.
If there is a shortage of space available to hold the food, the extra food is thrown out.
A shortage of fuel or labor may only affect *part* of the farms; ie, if there is enough fuel for 50,000 FRM's out of a total of 90,000 FRM's, then 40,000 FRM's will not produce.

### 8.1.3 TYPES OF FARMS

#### 8.1.3.1 FRM-1

TL-1 FRM's are like our modern dirt farms.
They may only be assembled in open colonies.

#### 8.1.3.2 FRM-2 through FRM-5

These are hydroponic farms.
They may be assembled in any colonies in orbits 1 through 5, but not in ships in any orbit, and not in colonies in orbits 6 - 11.

#### 8.1.3.3 FRM-6 through FRM-10

These use artificial sunlight and may be used in any s/c.

### 8.1.4 PERSONNEL REQUIRED

The personnel required to run one FRM unit of any TL is one PRO and three USK.

### 8.1.5 FUEL USE

One FRM-1 uses 0.5 fuel units per turn; one FRM-2 through FRM-5 uses 0.5 times the TL in fuel units per turn; one FRM-6 through FRM-11 uses 1 fuel unit times the TL per turn.
FRM's on OBC's in orbits 1-5 use no fuel, but use solar power.

### 8.1.6 SHUT DOWNS AND START UPS

Shut down orders (SHT) are used to stop the farming done by *all* assembled farms in a s/c; one can not shut down only a portion of them.
Start up orders (STU) are used ONLY to start up farms that have been shut down.
FRM's are assembled in running condition.

### 8.1.7 QUANTITY OF FARMS ALLOWED PER PLANET

The number of FRM-1 that can be assembled in the open colonies of a planet is limited to the habitability number of the planet times 100,000.
There is no limit to the number of FRM's TL-2 through TL-10 that can be assembled in open colonies.
And there is no limit to FRM's of any TL in ships, enclosed surface colonies, or orbiting colonies, providing there is enough space for them, and the restrictions in 8.1.3 are observed.

## 8.2 MINING

Mining is done by mine (MIN) units at deposits of natural resources.
The four types of natural resources are: fuel (FUEL), gold (GOLD), non-metallics (NMTS) and metallics (METS).
In order for a mine unit to work, it must be taken from storage and assembled into a MIN group.
(All MIN's in a group must be of the same TL.)
This is done with an ASM (assembly) order, and is explained in Chapter 9, Assembly.

### 8.2.1 AMOUNT OF RESOURCES MINED

Each deposit is rated for yield; the yield percentage indicates the percentage of usable resources that can be extracted from each unit of resources mined.
If a deposit had a 90% yield, and 100 units of resource were mined, 90 units of resources would be added to the player's resources in storage.
One MIN mines 25 times its TL in resource units per turn.

### 8.2.2 EFFECT OF SHORTAGES

If there is a shortage of available space to hold the amount of *gold* or *fuel* which would normally be mined on a turn, the MIN's will only mine the quantity which will fit the space available, and no more.
Shortages of space do not affect NMTS or METS: extra amounts of those are stored outside the colony walls.
(This applies only to enclosed surface colonies and open colonies.
Mines are not operable on ships or orbiting colonies).
Shortages of fuel or labor will cause MIN's to not produce, either fully or partially.
MIN groups produce one by one, starting with MIN group #1, then #2, etc.
As many mines will produce as are able before the shortage stops production.

### 8.2.3 FUEL AND PERSONNEL REQUIRED

Each MIN consumes 0.5 times its TL in fuel each turn.
Therefore, a MIN-3 consumes 1.5 fuel units each turn.
One MIN of any TL requires one PRO and three USK to operate it.

### 8.2.4 QUANTITY OF MINES ALLOWED

Only one s/c may have a MIN group at a deposit.
If two s/c's attempt to place MIN groups on the same deposit, one of them will be chosen randomly by the computer to succeed, and the other will fail.
A s/c may not have more than one MIN group at a deposit.
Only 40 mine groups are allowed in any enclosed surface colony, or in any open colony.
Since all deposits are located on planets, no MIN's can function on ships or orbiting colonies.
MIN's may be held in storage, however.

### 8.2.5 MCH, DEP, AND DAS ORDERS

When a player no longer needs a mine group, he disassembles it with a DAS (disassembly) order, and the mine units go automatically into storage.
This is explained fully in Chapter 9, Assembly.
Mine units may be moved from one deposit to another, turned off, or turned on with a mine change order (MCH).
Examples of MCH orders are shown in Chapter 17, Order Writing.
All MIN's at a deposit may be given to another s/c with a deposit gift (DEP) order.

## 8.3 MANUFACTURING

Manufacturing is done by factory (FCT) units.
In order for a factory unit to work, it must be taken from storage and assembled into a FCT group, as is explained in Chapter 9, Assembly.
All FCT's in a FCT group must be at the same TL.
Factory groups manufacture everything except population units, food, and resources.
Each FCT group can only manufacture one item at a time.
No item can be built in less than a year.
All FCT groups require four turns to produce any item in production.
There may be more than one FCT group building the same item.
A colony may have only 25 factory groups.
When a FCT group completes an item, it is placed in storage.
Ships can NOT have assembled factories (operating factories), but may carry them in storage.

### 8.3.1 FUEL AND PERSONNEL REQUIREMENTS

Each FCT requires one PRO times the efficiency factor (see 8.3.2) and three USK times the efficiency factor.
Each FCT uses 0.5 fuel units times the factory's TL per turn, except FCT units in OBC's within the 5th orbit or less, which use 0 fuel.
Personnel and fuel are only used by assembled (functioning) FCT groups.

### 8.3.2 EFFICIENCY FACTOR - COST

Small factory groups are less efficient than larger ones.
This is reflected by the group's efficiency factor, which determines the cost of building an item.
The quantity of METS and NMTS required to build an item, (see the back cover, UNIT CHARACTERISTICS SUMMARY), is multiplied by the FCT group's efficiency factor.
The resulting figure is multiplied by the quantity of the unit being manufactured.
This results in the cost per year; to determine the cost per turn, divide by four.
For instance, the cost of a LFS-2 is 6 METS and 10 NMTS.
The quantity of FCT's in the group is 50,000, so the efficiency factor is 1.
62,500 LFS-2 times 6 METS and 10 NMTS = 375,000 METS and 625,000 NMTS, (annual cost).
The cost per turn is 93,750 METS and 156,250 NMTS.

| Number of FCT's in Group | Efficiency Factor |
|--------------------------|-------------------|
| 1 ... 4                  | 6                 |
| 5 ... 49                 | 5                 |
| 50 ... 499               | 4                 |
| 500 ... 4,999            | 3                 |
| 5,000 ... 49,999         | 2                 |
| 50.000 ... 199,999       | 1                 |
| 200,000  ... infinity    | 0.5               |

The efficiency factor is not used when calculating the number of units a FCT group can produce.

    NOTE: In the MAXI-CHALLENGE game, the efficiency factor is always 1 when calculating cost per year, and is never less than 1 when calculating personnel requirements.

### 8.3.3 CAPACITY OF A FACTORY GROUP

One FCT (factory unit) produces 5 x its TL MU per turn; 20 MU x its TL per year.
This annual production in mass units, times the number of factory units in the group, divided by the mass units per item, will give the quantity of an item to be produced per year by a FCT group.
For example: A FCT group of 50,000 FCT-1 is set up to build LFS-2.
20 (mass units per year) times 50,000 (number of FCT units in the group) divided by 16 (a LFS-2 is 16 mass units) will equal 62,500 LFS-2 produced per year, or 15,625 LFS-2 produced per turn.

### 8.3.4 WORK IN PROCESS

Work in process (WIP) includes all items in the process of being manufactured.
Using the example in 8.3.3. of 50,000 FCT-1 being ASM'd (on turn 3) to build 62,500 LFS-2, the printout will report the WIP in this way:

| TURN | WIP 1/4 COMP. | WIP 1/2 COMP. | WIP 3/4 COMP. |
|------|---------------|---------------|---------------|
| 3    | 0             | 0             | 0             |
| 4    | 62,500 LFS-2  | 0             | 0             |
| 5    | 0             | 62,500 LFS-2  | 0             |
| 6    | 0             |               | 62,500 LFS-2  |

Note that the FCT group does not complete 1/4 of the LFS-2 per turn (15,625 in other words), but instead will get the entire 62,500 one quarter of the way to being complete.
On turn 7 of the example, 62,500 LFS-2 will show up on the storage report on the printout, and the WIP will look like it did on turn 3.

Let's say that we assembled this group on turn three, and assembled more FCT units to the group on turn 4, and turn 5 and turn 6.
The printout will show:

| TURN | WIP1/4 COMP. | WIP 1/2 COMP. | WIP 3/4 COMP. |
|------|--------------|---------------|---------------|
| 3    | 0            | 0             | 0             |
| 4    | 62,500 LFS-2 | 0             | 0             |
| 5    | 62,500 LFS-2 | 62,500 LFS-2  | 0             |
| 6    | 62,500 LFS-2 | 62,500 LFS-2  | 62,500 LFS-2  |

Starting on turn 7, 62,500 LFS-2 are added to storage each turn.

### 8.3.5 BCH, COM, AND DAS ORDERS

Once ordered to manufacture an item, a factory group will continue to do so, unless ordered to build something else with a build change order (BCH - see 8.3.6, Retooling), or to shut down (a BCH, SHT order).
Once ordered to shut down, a FCT group (still assembled) remains shut down, unless ordered to start up (with a BCH, STU order).
One final way to stop a factory from producing is to disassemble it with a DAS (dis-assembly) order.
This is explained in Chapter 9, Assembly.
Dis-assembled factory units go into storage.
A combine (COM) order combines two factory groups into one; both groups must be manufacturing the same item.

### 8.3.6 RETOOLING

A FCT group may be changed from building one item to building another, but it must retool first with a BCH, RETL order, with the following exceptions.

* In the case of switching from making an item to making research points, no retool order is needed (switching from research to an item *does* require a retool order.)

* Also, in the case of switching from one type of structural unit (STUN, LTSU, SLSU) to another, no retool order is needed.
* If the FCT units in a group are occupied with WIP, they will not retool, even though the BCH, RETL order is accepted and acknowledged on the printout.
  As the FCT are freed from WIP, they retool themselves.

In order to determine the number of FCT that will be retooled on a given turn, determine the total mass of all the items in WIP as if they were complete, and divide by 20 times the TL of the FCT.
Subtract this result from the quantity of FCT in the FCT group; the result is the number of FCT that will be retooled on a given turn.
A FCT group must retool when changing to building the same item at a higher TL.
Retooling uses 0.01 METS and 0.01 NMTS per FCT in the group being retooled.
Retooled FCT's are shown in WIP.

### 8.3.7 RESEARCH AND TL

A factory group ordered to build research (RSCH) will build one research point per FCT times the FCT's TL per game year, divided by four for the amount per turn.
A FCT group may not build any item of a higher TL than that of the s/c it is in.
RSCH and TL's are further explained in Chapter 10, Technological Levels.

### 8.3.8 STUN, LTSU, AND SLSU

STUN are only built in ESC's and OPC's.
STUN can not be built in OBC's.
An attempt to do so will result in the computer changing the STUN to either LTSU or SLSU.

* OBC's TL 1-4 build LTSU, so the computer would change the STUN to LTSU.
* OBC's TL5-10 build SLSU, so the computer would change the STUN to SLSU.
* And if one tried to have an OBC-7, for instance, build LTSU, the computer would automatically change the LTSU to SLSU.

In other words, ordering a heavier type of structural unit than what should have been ordered will cause the computer to correct it to the appropriate lighter type.
But ordering a *lighter* structural unit than what should have been ordered will cause the order to abort.

### 8.3.9 SHORTAGES AND FACTORY GROUP NUMBERS

FCT groups will move as many items through its WIP as there are resources, labor, and fuel to do so.
The computer processes the manufacturing group by group in *group number order*, and within each group starting with WIP 3/4, WIP 1/2, then WIP 1/4.
When the shortage occurs, the computer will cease to work on the portion it was processing, and not do any more processing.
If the shortage has been taken care of on the next turn, the computer will process as normal, starting with FCT group #1, WIP 3/4.
If the shortage still exists, processing will not be done.
There is no way for a player to change a particular FCT group number of a FCT group to another number; if there is a shortage, the player should issue a BCH, SHT to his less necessary FCT groups.
Although the player can not assign a group # to a FCT group when it is initially assembled, he can determine what number the computer assigns to it.
The computer assigns #1 to the first FCT group ASM order it encounters, #2 to the second and so on.
If a player DAS's a FCT group, that number remains unassigned until a new ASM is ordered.

### 8.3.10 AUTOMATION

Automation units (AUT) are used to replace USK for FRM's, MIN's, and FCT's.
The AUT's TL squared times the number of AUT's being used equals the number of USKs being replaced.
AUT's are not assigned to specific production groups, but are counted as USK labor available to any production group in the s/c.
If a colony, for instance, has 1,000 FRM's, 10,000 MIN's, and one 50,000 factory unit group, it will need 183,000 USK or the equivalent in AUT (which could be 183,000 AUT-1 or 45,750 AUT-2 or 7,320 AUT-5, etc.).
AUT's use no fuel.

# 9. ASSEMBLY

## 9.1 ASSEMBLY AND DISASSEMBLY

Assembly (ASM) orders are used in two ways: to assemble special purpose population groups (CNW's and SPYS), and to assemble unassembled items.

### 9.1.1 ASSEMBLING SPECIAL PURPOSE POPULATION GROUPS

When an ASM order is given for a SPYS cadre, 1 PRO unit and 1 SLD unit are taken from their population segments and joined to form one group spy cadre.
When an ASM order is given for a CNW cadre, 1 PRO unit and 1 USK unit are taken from their population segments and formed into one construction worker cadre.

### 9.1.2 ASSEMBLING UNASSEMBLED ITEMS

Unassembled items are "assembly type" items that have been just manufactured or disassembled.
Assembly type items work only when they have been assembled with an ASM (assembly) order; they are called assembled items when assembled.
When they have been disassembled with a DAS (dis-assembly) order, the items do not function, and they are then called unassembled items.
*Unassembled items go into storage automatically*.

The following items are assembly type items:

* AUT
* ESH
* EWP
* FCT
* FRM
* HDRV
* LFS
* LTSU
* MIN
* MSL
* SEN
* SDRV
* STUN
* SLSU

An ASM order takes an unassembled item from storage, assembles it, and makes it work.

### 9.1.3 DISASSEMBLING ASSEMBLED ITEMS

Dis-assembly (DAS) orders are used to reverse assembly orders.
That is, they disassemble items and put them into storage, and are also used to break up SPYS and CNW's.

    Note: only group G SPYS can be DAS'd.

Dis-assembly of non-population units causes a 10% *LOSS*.
For instance, if 5,000 EWP-2 are dis-assembled then 500 will be lost and 4,500 will appear in storage under "un-assembled items" in the storage section of the printout.
There is no loss in disassembling population units.
When determining the 10% loss, fractions are rounded down.

### 9.1.4 NON-ASSEMBLY ITEMS

Along with unassembled items (UNA), non-assembly items (NON) are also kept in storage.
NON, however, perform their functions without being assembled.
NON can not be assembled or dis-assembled.

The following items are NON:

* TPT
* MSS
* METS
* NMTS
* FUEL
* FOOD
* ASW
* ASC
* ANM
* CNGD
* MRB
* MTSP

NON are taken by the computer from storage automatically as used per turn.

### 9.1.5 USE OF CONSTRUCTION WORKERS

Dis-assembly requires 1 CNW per 500 mass units being dis-assembled.
Assembly requires 1 CNW per 500 mass units being assembled per turn.
If a s/c has 5,000 EWP-2 in storage, it would require 200 CNW ((20 x 5000) ÷ 500) to ASM them.

### 9.1.6 REGENTS

A Regent is allowed to ASM spies, but not to issue spy movement (SPM) orders.
All spies assembled by a Regent will be under the control of his Ruler.

### 9.1.7 MISCELLANEOUS

Assembled items take up TWICE as many volume units as when unassembled.
Also, assembled items do not leave the s/c.
Only UNA or NON can be TFR'd or ADN'd to another s/c, or can be picked up by another s/c.
So, assembled items must be dis-assembled first.
Mine units are assembled on a particular deposit.
This assembly causes the MIN's to begin functioning automatically; a start-up order is not necessary.
Factory units are assembled into factory groups; causing the FCT units to begin functioning automatically; no start-up order is necessary.

## 9.2 SCRAP

Scrap (SCR) orders convert items *in storage* back into resource units.
Scrapping causes a 30% loss.
For instance, scrapping 10,000 MSS-1 will result in 14,000 NMTS and 14,000 METS.
Scrapping requires one CNW per 300 MU scrapped.

Example: Dis-assembly of 50,000 MU, scrapping of 3,000 MU, and assembly of 100.000 mass units will require a total of 310 CNW units.
(CNW can not be used twice during the same turn).

Scrapping of population units is not allowed.

# 10. TECHNOLOGICAL LEVELS

## 10.1 SHIPS AND COLONIES

Home colonies (nations) start at TL-1 and advance through the levels (1 through 10) in consecutive order.
The TL of a colony determines the highest TL unit that can be manufactured by the factories in a colony, and determines the highest *TL* that is sold or transferred by that colony.
A ship's TL determines the highest TL that the ship can transfer.
A s/c's TL does not limit the TL of the items existing in the s/c's themselves.
When a s/c is conquered, it always loses one TL, but TL's never decrease to less than one.

    A conquered colony continues manufacturing items at the same TL, but if any change was ordered, or if more FCT were assembled into the group, the units made would be at the lesser TL.

The s/c's TL is not referred to on the player's turnsheet, but is shown in the Vital Statistics portion of each s/c report on the printout.

## 10.2 MANUFACTURING

A FCT's TL is not determined by a s/c's TL, and it does not affect the TL of an item being manufactured, it only affects the FCT's output.
For instance, a TL-5 colony can have TL-9 FCT's it bought, but they can not manufacture items of a TL higher than 5.

    Reminder: Ships do not have functioning factories.

This colony is able to sell or transfer the FCT-9's, but it would only be able to sell or transfer a technological level of 5.
More about transferring and selling TL's and RSCH in section 10.4, IMPROVING TL.

## 10.3 UNIT TL

Once a unit has been manufactured, (any unit - even FCT's), its TL can not be changed.
Therefore, if a s/c wishes to upgrade the TL of its FCT's, ASW, etc, it must manufacture, buy or trade for the higher-TL items, and decide what should be done with the lower-TL items.
As stated before, the TL of a FCT or a s/c does not limit the TL of the items in the s/c, a TL-1 s/c can have TL-5 FCT's, TL-8 TPT's, etc.

All units have a TL except for the following:

* POP
* CNGD
* FOOD
* GOLD
* FUEL
* METS
* NMTS
* MTSP
* STUN
* LTSU
* SLSU

*A unit's TL is part of the unit's name.*

SEN-1 are very different from SEN-10 in mass, fuel consumption, and capabilities.
When writing orders, the player must always include the TL on items that need it; otherwise his orders may fail to be processed.
A player is not restricted to having one TL per item; that is, he could have SEN-1, SEN-5, and SEN-10 all in the same s/c at the same time.

## 10.4 IMPROVING TL

Only a s/c's TL can be improved.
There are three ways to do so.

### 10.4.1 RESEARCH POINTS

One way to improve a s/c's TL is to build the required amount of research points necessary to raise it.
A build change - research - order (BCH, RSCH) will cause a FCT group to change from manufacturing an item to building RSCH.
A FCT producing RSCH will build .25 times its TL in RSCH per turn.
The FCT group will complete its work-in-process before beginning the research.
RSCH may be transferred or sold, decreasing the total number of RSCH accumulated.
Once the required number of RSCH have been built, the computer automatically raises the TL of the colony, and decreases the number of RSCH accumulated.
The quantities of RSCH listed below for each level are the number required to get from the preceeding level to the one listed:

| LEVEL | RSCH       |
|-------|------------|
| 2     |    100,000 |
| 3     |    200,000 |
| 4     |    400,000 |
| 5     |    800,000 |
| 6     |  1,600,000 |
| 7     |  3,200,000 |
| 8     |  6,400,000 |
| 9     | 12,800,000 |
| 10    | 25,600,000 |
    
A REC order is not written by the recipient of a RSCH transfer.
Note that METS and NMTS are not required for the production of RSCH.

### 10.4.2 TRANSFERRING RSCH

The second way to improve a s/c's TL is to transfer RSCH from another s/c.
A s/c transferring RSCH will have that number deducted from the total RSCH accumulated.

When a s/c's established, it automatically starts out with TL-1, whether the establishing s/c has a higher TL or not.

TFR'ing RSCH requires TPT's, but RSCH do not use up any TPT capacity (see 11.1).

### 10.4.3 BUYING AND SELLING RSCH

The third way to improve a s/c's TL is to buy technology (RSCH) on the market.
A s/c selling RSCH will have that number deducted from the total RSCH accumulated.

# 11. TRANSFERS

## 11.1 TRANSFERS AND PICK-UPS

A transfer (TFR) order moves units from one s/c to another, using transports (TPT's) of the sending s/c.
A pick-up (PUP) order moves units from one s/c to another, using TPT's of the receiving s/c.
Any item may be TFR'd or PUP'd if both s/c's belong to the same Ruler, or a Ruler and his Regent, or two Regents belonging to the same Ruler.
If a s/c belongs to another Ruler of the same race, or his Regent, any item may be TFR'd to it if the receiving s/c writes a receive (REC) order, and any item may be PUP'd from it if that s/c writes an allow pick-up (APU) order.
If a s/c belongs to a Ruler of another race, or his Regent, only gold may be TFR'd to it, and nothing may be PUP'd from it.
Gold and RSCH do not require a REC order (see 11.5).
Spies are never traded and never change hands.
Group G spies may be TFR'd into any s/c, and PUP'd back out of it, to conduct espionage; they remain under their Ruler's control (see 19.2).
(A reminder: spies assembled by a Regent are controlled by his Ruler - they become the Ruler's own spies).

## 11.2 TRANSPORTS

PUP's and TFR's are done by transports (TPT's).
They also do surveys, ADN's, STP's, jettisons, and move troops in combat.
TPT's are not capable of making trips between planets.
They are only used to move goods and people between s/c's at the same orbit.
TPT's have their own life support capabilities; it is not necessary to provide food or LFS for POP being moved in TPT's.

When not in combat, 10 TPT's can be operated by 1 PRO.
When used in combat, TPT's are operated by the SLD's they carry.

### 11.2.1 TRANSPORT CAPACITY

TPT capacity is the mass units a TPT can carry.
To determine TPT capacity, a player multiplies the TPT's TL squared times 200.
For instance, a TPT-4 will have a capacity of 3,200 MU.
RSCH TFR's do not use up TPT capacity.

### 11.2.2 TRANSFERRING TRANSPORTS

TPT's being transferred or picked up simply move themselves from one s/c to another.
They are not put inside another TPT, and therefore do not use any TPT capacity, nor do they carry anything inside them.
The TPT's do all their work before being TFR'd or PUP'd.
The s/c that has the TPT's to start with gets full use of them on the turn they are TFR'd; the new owner will not be able to use them until the next turn.

## 11.3 REBEL PICK-UPS AND TRANSFERS

Pickups of POP *never* include rebels.
If there are not enough loyal POP to make up the quantity specified in the order, then the amount picked up is reduced.
TFR's of POP *always* include rebels, unless the X option is used.
See Chapter 18, Rebellion, for further details regarding rebels.

### 11.3.1 X OPTION

If the X option is used, only loyal POP will transfer.
The X option is used in TFR's only, *never in any other order*.
In order to TFR loyal POP only, change the last letter of the POP to X.
For example, PRO would be PRX, SLD would be SLX, CNW would be CNX, etc.

## 11.4 SHORTAGES

Shortages of fuel or PRO's will result in all or part of the TFR or PUP orders failing.
TFR's and PUP's are done in the order in which they are listed on the turnsheet; they are done one by one until stopped by a shortage.

    NOTE: NEVER WRITE MORE THAN ONE TFR OR PUP ORDER FOR THE SAME ITEM TO THE SAME S/C ON THE SAME TURN, ie: 30, TFR, 40,000, METS, 26. and 30, TFR, 80,000, METS, 26.

This is very important as doing this can not only cause the order to abort, but can cause other errors in the turn, as well.
The order will also abort if there is not enough space in the receiving s/c to house the new items.

## 11.5 RSCH

RSCH are transferred, but are never picked up.
REC orders are unnecessary for them.
They do not use up any TPT capacity.
All other rules concerning TFR's apply to them.

## 11.6 JETTISONS

When a unit is jettisoned, it is dumped into space.
This is done by transferring the unit to s/c "0".

    HOME COLONIES MAY NEVER JETTISON THEIR POP.

Any type of POP may otherwise be jettisoned.
If POP's are jettisoned, then the rebels in that s/c will increase by 10 times the amount of POP jettisoned.
For example assume that 500 SLD are jettisoned, and the colony had 1,000 rebels.
10 times 500 (SLD) is 5,000.
5,000 is added to 1,000 rebels, making a total of 6,000 rebels in that colony.

# 12. MARKET

## 12.1 FOR ALL GAMES OF EMPYREAN CHALLENGE

### 12.1.1 WHO GETS THE MARKET REPORT

At the beginning of the game, there is a market on each home planet.
Each member of the home planet's race will get a market report for that planet.
Players with a s/c at a "foreign" planet will get a market report for that planet.
To establish a market on a planet other than the player's home planet, he must first set up a colony there, (not a ship), and then write a SEL order.
At that point, the market report will show up in his printout for that colony.

### 12.1.2 HOW THE MARKET WORKS

The market sells items from a stockpile of items it collects.
At the beginning of the game, it only has NMTS, METS, and FUEL in its stockpile, but as it buys items from the players, those items will be for sale.
All units in the game, even population units, can be sold, except for: SPYS and TL's.
Only NON and UNA can be sold; assembled items must be DAS'd first before they can be sold.
The units are sold on a highest-bidder-come-first basis.
If more items are bought by the market than sold, the surplus is added to the market's stockpile of these items.
When an item is out of stock, that item is taken off the market's list of items that turn.
To get an item back on the list or to get a new TL item on the list, a sell (SEL) order for that item must be written.
The market will establish a starting price and place the item on its list.

### 12.1.3 PRICES

On each printout in the News Service, there will be a market report that will list the price the market will pay for each item and the price it will take in payment for each item it has for sale.
Prices will vary from turn to turn and are based on the bids, quantities sold and quantities bought, and also on the market's gold supply.
If the market is short of gold, it will keep its minimum selling prices high enough to recover enough gold.
If the market has an excess of gold, it will actually sell for less than it pays.
The actual method of determining new prices and changing prices is known only to the computer.

#### 12.1.3.1 Price of Unemployables

The price of UEM's alway's starts as a negative (-) number; that is, the player pays the market to take them.

    No other items may be priced below 0.

It is even possible that the market will pay to have UEM's taken off its hands.

### 12.1.4 BIDS

The market sells units to the highest bidder, any left over go to the second highest, etc.
Players who wish to bid write a buy order (BUY) for a certain quantity of an item, at X number of gold units apiece.
A fraction of a gold unit may be used.
If a player's BUY price does not exceed the market's selling price, the order will be ignored.
A player sells items to the market with sell (SEL) orders, in which no price is listed, as the units can only be sold at the market's buying price.

## 12.2 HOME MARKET (DELETED)

    Deleted.

# 13. SHIP MOVEMENT

## 13.1 HYPER DRIVES

Hyper drives (HDRV) are used to propel ships through hyper space, either within a solar system or from one system to another.
The engine's jump range is one light year times its TL.
A ship may have as many HDRV as desired, but all HDRV on one ship should be at the same TL.
(If different TL's are used, the computer will use the lowest TL in computing the jump range).
Each HDRV can propel 1,045 mass units times its TL.
If the mass of the ship exceeds this, the ship will not respond to move (MOV) or jump (JMP) orders.
In calculating the number of HDRV necessary to propel the ship, the mass of the HDRV's are always counted.
Ships with less than 100 HDRV, regardless of the size of the ship, will not respond to MOV or JMP orders.
Also, if a ship does not have enough fuel to complete a MOV or JMP order, the ship will not respond to that order.
(In other words, a partial MOV or JMP will not be made).
Only the HDRV required by the mass of the ship will use fuel.
A minimum of 100 HDRV are always used.

## 13.2 SPACE DRIVES

A ship must have at least one space drive in order to establish itself in a stable orbit around a planet.
A ship without a space drive establishes itself in a decaying orbit which has a 50% chance of terminating each turn.
Other than the one space drive used to maintain orbit, space drives are not used for ship movement, per se.
They are used in combat, however, See 15.9.2 for details.
(Fuel is used by space drives only during combat).

## 13.3 MOVEMENT

Ships never land.
Transfers of POP and other units are done by TPT's.
There are two ways in which a ship may be propelled.
It can move (with a MOV order), or it can jump (with a JMP order).
Ships can only go to coordinate points where there is a solar system.

### 13.3.1 JUMPS

Jumps are not made within a solar system; they are only made from one system to another.
When a JMP order is written, a ship may jump from any orbit in a system to orbit 0 in another system.
(To move from orbit 0 to another orbit, the player writes a MOV order).
Each jump requires one turn to complete.
The distances in jumps are measured in light years.
To find the distance between one system and another, a system probe is made.
(See Chapter 14 for more information).

#### 13.3.1.1 Over-jumping

If a ship is ordered to JMP more than its HDRVs' TL in light years, there is a chance that either the HDRV will melt (still completing the JMP), or the HDRV will blow up (destroying the ship), or that the ship will arrive completely intact.
The following chart shows the odds.

##### 13.3.1.1.1 Chart

| LIGHT YEARS TO BE JUMPED DIVIDED BY THE HYPER DRIVES' TL  | CHANCE OF BLOW UP | CHANCE OF MELTING |
|-----------------------------------------------------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| 1 OR LESS                                                 | 0%                | 0%                |
| 2 OR LESS                                                 | 10%               | 20%               |
| 3 OR LESS                                                 | 20%               | 35%               |
| 4 OR LESS                                                 | 30%               | 50%               |
| 5 OR LESS                                                 | 40%               | 65%               |
| 6 OR LESS                                                 | 50%               | 80%               |
| 7 OR LESS                                                 | 60%               | 90%               |
| 8 OR LESS                                                 | 70%               | 100%              |
| 9 OR LESS                                                 | 80%               | 100%              |
| 10 OR LESS                                                | 90%               | 100%              |

### 13.3.2 MOVES

Moves can be either interplanetary or interstellar.
When moving from one orbit to another, the ship always arrives in the orbit between 5 and 10 distance units from the planet, and is informed of the ID# and name of the colony which controls the planet.
In the case of a home planet, the ship will be informed that a race controls it.
Interplanetary moves are always counted as 0.1 light years in distance, for purposes of fuel usage, and only take one turn.
A ship when MOVing is considered to be in hyper space; that is, time does not pass in the ship.
Neither food nor consumer goods are consumed by the crew; no fuel is used for life support.
Fuel for the trip is used up at the start of the trip.

#### 13.3.2.1 Interstellar Moves

A MOV from one system to another, can, and usually does, take more than one turn.
The advantage to MOVing, as opposed to jumping, is that there is no danger of the ship being destroyed.
No orders can be given to the ship until the ship arrives at its destination.
To compute the length of time it will take a ship to move to a new system, the number of light years between systems is divided by the lowest TL of the assembled HDRV on the ship.
If there is a fraction in the amount of light years, the number of turns is rounded up.
(10.6 light years divided by 3 (HDRV-3) = 3.53 turns, or 4 turns).

### 13.3.3 COMBAT (DELETED)

    Deleted.

### 13.3.4 FUEL USE

HDRV use 40 x the distance in light years in FUEL units.
When moving from one orbit to another orbit of the same star, the distance used is .1 light years.
When moving from an orbit of one star to the orbit of another star in the same system the distance used is .2 light years.

# 14. SURVEYS AND PROBES

## 14.1 SURVEYS

A survey (SUR) will report the number of natural resource deposits on a planet, their deposit location numbers, the resource each deposit contains, quantity of resource per deposit, and the planet's habitability number.
A survey also reports the number of TL-1 farms assembled and the total population of the open colonies there.
A SUR can only be made if the planet and s/c are in the same orbit.
A SUR is ordered with a survey (SUR) order, is completed in one turn, and requires one TPT and one PRO to do it.

## 14.2 PROBES

There are three types of probes that may be made.
All three are ordered with a probe (PRB) order.

### 14.2.1 ORBIT PROBES

An orbit probe lists the s/c's at the planet,the log base 10 of the total mass of each kind of resource in the unmined deposits on the planet, the log base 10 of each s/c's mass, its ID#, its name, the type of planet and its habitability number.
An orbit probe cannot be made of the orbit the s/c is in because s/c's automatically get a probe report of the orbit they are in every turn.
An orbit probe can be made of any other orbit in the system; in a multiple star system, all orbits in the systems can be probed from one orbit.
The orbit probe also lists the tactical coordinate positions of the s/c's at the planet.

#### 14.2.1.1 Log Base 10

| Mass                | Log Base 10 |
|---------------------|-------------|
| 0-9                 | 0           |
| 10-99               | 1           |
| 100-999             | 2           |
| 1,000-9,999         | 3           |
| 10,000-99,999       | 4           |
| 100,000-999,999     | 5           |
| 1,000,000-9,999,999 | 6           |
| and so on           |             |

### 14.2.2 S/C PROBES

A s/c probe lists the log base 10 the mass of a s/c, its population, its assembled STUN, LTSU, or SLSU, and its structural requirements.
Both the s/c doing the probing and the s/c being probed must be in the same orbit.

### 14.2.3 SYSTEM PROBES

A system probe will give the planet types by orbit; and a list of coordinates and distances of all the stars within a number of light years, depending on the magnitude of the probe.
Each magnitude equals one light year.
A magnitude 3 probe can probe 3 light years.
The maximum magnitude is 10.
The distances given by a system probe are the distances from the system the probing s/c is in.
The coordinates given are the coordinates *relative* to the home system.
The planet types reported are only for planets in the same system as the probing s/c.

## 14.3 SENSORS

A sensor (SEN) is the instrument that performs a probe.
The number of probes a s/c can perform per turn is limited to the number of SEN's it has assembled x their individual TL's.
The ratio of SEN's to s/c's probed is one to one; the ratio of sensors to orbits probed is one to one.
There is no magnitude for orbit and s/c probes.
The number of SEN's x the SEN's TL determines the maximum magnitude of a probe (up to 10).
A magnitude 10 probe would use 10 SEN-1 or 2 SEN-5 or 1 SEN-10, for example.
The total mass of a SEN is 3,000 times its TL.
The total fuel used per turn per SEN is its TL divided by 20.
As long as a s/c has one POP, of any type, that is considered enough to run the SEN.
If a s/c has 3 SEN-5, it would be able to execute 1 magnitude 5 system probe, 5 orbit orbit probes, and 5 s/c probes; or 1 magnitude 10 system probe and 5 s/c probes or any other combination that uses 15 probes.

# 15. COMBAT

## 15.1 COMBAT OPTIONS

### 15.1.1 INVADE

Attempt to take control of another s/c using troops (SLD and/or MRB).
This is done with an invade (INV) order.

### 15.1.2 RAID

Attempt to steal items from another s/c using troops.
This is done with a raid (RAD) order.

### 15.1.3 SUPPORT

Send troops either to help conquer or to defend another s/c.
This is done with an offensive troop support (OTS) order or defensive troop support (DTS) order, respectively.

### 15.1.4 WITHDRAW

Remove support from a battle, or as an invader, to end an invasion.
This is done with a withdraw (WTD) order.

### 15.1.5 BOMBARD

Fire energy weapons (EWP) and missile launchers (MSL) at other s/c's.
Bombardment is done the following orders: pre-maneuver energy weapon fire (PME), pre-maneuver missile fire (PMM), after-maneuver energy weapon fire (AME), and after maneuver missile fire (AMM).

### 15.1.6 MANEUVER

Change the position of ships around a planet, using the ship's space drives.
It is done with the following orders: tactical maneuver (TMV), close (CLS), and run (RUN) orders.

## 15.2 INVASION

In the invade (INV) order, the Player lists the material and personnel that are to be used in the invasion.
Only troops (soldiers and military robots), assault craft (ASC), and assault weapons (ASW) can be listed in an INV order.
The troops should be written first in the order, followed by the ASC and/or ASW, if needed.

### 15.2.1 ASSAULT WEAPONS

Assault weapons (ASW) are something like a cannon; they are very powerful, but they are not self propelled.
They must be carried to the assigned s/c in TPTs.
They may be used to invade, support, raid, or defend open colonies and enclosed surface colonies *only*.
Each ASW requires one soldier (SLD) or military robot (MRB) equivalent to operate it.
The TPT's required to carry the ASW into battle are not written in the order.
The computer automatically assigns the necessary TPT's to carry them.
If there are not enough TPT's, then that number of ASW will not participate.
Troops can not use ASW to INV ships or orbiting colonies.

### 15.2.2 ASSAULT CRAFT

Assault craft (ASC) are armed vehicles that not only carry troops into battle, but are formidable weapons, as well.
They may be used to INV, raid, support, or defend any type of s/c.
ASC require one SLD or MRB equivalent to operate a unit.
ASC do not require TPT's to move them into combat; they are completely mobil.
When invading or raiding ships, the ASC's speed must be greater than the ship's speed.
An ASC's speed is 5 times its TL.
If the number of ASC is insufficient to hold the MRB and SLD, the computer will assign enough TPT's to carry the balance.
The TPT's are not written in a combat order.
ASC's use fuel when sent into battle, and then use fuel each turn the battle lasts.

#### 15.2.2.1 Transports-Fuel

TPT's are not fired upon in an invasion.
They are assumed to drop troops, or troops and ASW, at a distance from the s/c.
The TPT's then return to base.
In combat, each TPT can move 3 x its TL^2 in mass units per turn.
Thus, ten SLD armed with ten ASW-1 require the use of 10 TPT-1.
If there are not enough ASC, or TPT's, or fuel for either, the troops will not go.
The computer processes the ASC and ASW (in an order) in the order in which they are written, so in the case of a shortage, the player should keep this in mind.
TPT's used in combat are still able to be used in transfer type operations.
TPT capacity is not used up in invade or raid orders.

### 15.2.3 TROOPS

Troops consist of soldiers (SLD) and military robots (MRB).
One SLD can operate one ASC *or* one ASW at the same time, but not both.
A MRB can operate two times its TL in ASC *and/or* ASW.
Troops can only use "small arms" (see MTSP) or ASC to invade ships or orbiting colonies.
Troops can use "small arms", or ASC, or ASW, or both ASW and ASC to invade enclosed surface colonies or open colonies.

## 15.3 RAIDS

RAD orders are written the same as INV orders, with the exception that troops are ordered to carry off as much as they can of the assigned items.
Each SLD can carry off one mass unit; MRB carry 2 mass units times their TL per unit.
Raiders take only NON or UNA, never assembled units.
They also take POP, with the exception of spies.
They will not raid for TL's or RSCH.

## 15.4 SUPPORT

Invaders or defenders in an INV may receive supporting forces from other s/c's.
These s/c's may be other s/c's belonging to that Ruler or his Regent or may belong to a different Ruler or Regent.
The supporting forces fight for the s/c designated in the order.
If a force is supporting an invading force, and the invader's force is withdrawn, then the supporting force will be forced to withdraw.
Should a force (supporting or otherwise) be forced to withdraw with no place to withdraw *to*, (their s/c being destroyed, captured, or moved), it must surrender.
This applies to a force *ordered* to withdraw, as well.
A defensive support order, besides supporting a player against invasion will support him against raids, as well.
But an offensive support order does not include raids.
Raiders get no support.
If troops are supporting, they will use MTSP & fuel whether or not the invasion takes place.
This applies to both offensive & defensive support in an invasion, and to defensive support in a raid.

## 15.5 WITHDRAWAL

Invading or supporting players may withdraw all or part of their forces on any turn if they have a s/c at the same orbit to withdraw to.
If they do not, they are forced to surrender.
WTD's use TPT capacity just as transfer type orders do.

## 15.6 BOMBARDMENT

### 15.6.1 TYPES OF BOMBARDMENT ORDERS

#### 15.6.1.1 Pre-Maneuver Energy Weapon Fire

The pre-maneuver energy weapon fire (PME) order has a s/c fire a percentage of its energy weapons (EWP's) at a certain s/c before ship maneuvers are processed in the combat execution sequence.
A particular target within the s/c may be chosen by the player, if desired.
This is explained in 15.6.2, Targets.

#### 15.6.1.2 Pre-Maneuver Missile Fire

The pre-maneuver missile fire (PMM) order has a s/c fire a percentage of its MSL's at a certain s/c before ship maneuvers are processed in the combat execution sequence.
A particular target may be chosen within the s/c.

#### 15.6.1.3 After-Maneuver Energy Weapon Fire.

The After-maneuver energy weapon fire (AME) order has a s/c fire a percentage of its energy weapons at a certain s/c after ship maneuvers are processed in the combat execution sequence.
Again, a particular target may be chosen.

#### 15.6.1.4 After-Maneuver Missile Fire.

The after-maneuver missile fire (AMM) order has s/c fire a percentage of its MSL's at a certain s/c after ship maneuvers are processed in the combat execution sequence.
A target within the s/c may be chosen.

### 15.6.2 TARGETS

A player does not have to specify a target in his bombardment orders.
If targets are not specified, the damage will be done randomly.
Specifying a target will increase the chance of hitting the target area by 50%.
Only colonies may be targeted for bombardment, not ships.

#### 15.6.2.1 Types of Targets

Each type is represented by a numeral.

1. Population (randomly selected, including spies).
2. Non-assembly and unassembled units.
3. Assembled units.
4. Mine groups
5. Factory groups

### 15.6.3 MISSILES AND MISSILE LAUNCHERS

Missiles (MSS) are fired by missile launchers (MSL); one MSS per MSL per firing.

There are two firings per turn - pre-maneuver, and after-maneuver.
A MSS does 100 x its TL-squared mass units in damage.
A MSS may be fired by a MSL of the same TL only.
MSS's are fired by ships or colonies at ships or colonies in the same orbit.

#### 15.6.3.1 Missile Hits

The number of missile hits per turn is calculated as follows:

    H = M /(D + ( SP /10) -S)^2

Where H is the number of hits, M is the number of MSS fired, D is the distance units to the target, SP is the speed of the target and S is the TL of the highest TL sensor on board.
The number of hits may be further reduced by anti-missiles and EWP.
If the expression, "D+ (SP/10)-S", is less than 1, the value one will be used instead.

### 15.6.4 ANTI-MISSILES

The number of anti-missiles (ANM) that can be fired per *bombardment* equals the number of assembled MSL in the s/c.
ANM's are only used defensively; they are fired at each bombardment on a s/c.
This is not written in an order; it is done automatically by the computer.
ANM are fired by MSL, and those MSL must be of the same TL as the ANM.

#### 15.6.4.1 Example

If 3 s/c's fire MSS at a s/c which has 10,000 MSL-1 and 50,000 ANM-1; and if attacker A fired 5,000 MSS, attacker B fired 10,000 MSS, and attacker C fired 15,000 MSS, then the defender would fire *up to* 10,000 ANM to stop each volley of MSS.
That is, 9,091 to stop all of A's, and 10,000 each at B's and C's.
The distance between the attacking colonies was one distance unit, and all ANM's, MSS's, and MSL's were TL-1, in this example.

#### 15.6.4.2 Used against an invasion

Besides defending against bombardment, as explained above, ANM's are also used defending against invasions and raids.
This also is automatic, and does not require an order from the player.
The number of ANM that can be fired *per turn* of an invasion equals the number of assembled MSL in the s/c.
The ANM's are fired only once per turn at *all* of the attacking forces combined, the hits are distributed among them proportionately, according to each attacker's combat factor.
When a s/c faces a bombardment and invasion both, the MSL's fire ANM's at each volley of incoming MSS's, as well as once at all the invading troops.

#### 15.6.4.3 Anti-missile Hits

Hits made by ANM's are determined as follows: HP = the quantity, 2 to the power of the ANM's TL divided by 2 to the power of the MSS's TL, times .55.
HP is the fraction of ANM that hit.
The formula is written thus: HP =(2^AT ÷ 2^MT) x .55
HP is the fraction of ANM that hit, AT is the ANM TL, MT is the TL of the MSS.
In the case of an invasion, MT is the TL of their ASC or ASW.
If a force is not armed with either ASC or ASW, then MT = 1.

### 15.6.5 ENERGY WEAPONS AND ENERGY SHIELDS

#### 15.6.5.1 Offense

Energy weapons (EWP) are fired by s/c's at s/c's in the same orbit.
EWP's fire a beam of energy and are not mobile.
The damage is calculated as follows:

    DA = ((F ÷ (D + SP/10 - S)) x E) - SH

Where DA is the damage in mass units, F is the number of EWP that fire, D is the number of distance units to the target, SP is the speed, S is the highest TL sensor assembled in the attacker's s/c, E is the energy of each unit fired (10 x TL of the EWP), SH is the target's shielding.
(Shielding is the total energy units deflected by all of the assembled energy shields in the s/c.
Each energy shield (ESH) deflects 10 x its TL-squared energy units per attack).
If the expression D + SP/10 - S is less than 1, the value 1 will be used in its place to solve the rest of the equation.
The firing of EWP against s/c's happens twice per turn: pre-maneuver and after-maneuver.

    EWP MAY NOT FIRE FROM A SURFACE COLONY AT A SURFACE COLONY.

EWP's use fuel each time they are fired.

#### 15.6.5.2 Defense

EWP's are also used for defense against raiders, invaders and attacking MSS's.
This is automatic, the computer handles it, it is never ordered.
EWP's fire at each volley of MSS that were not destroyed by ANM's.
EWP's fire only once per turn against invaders and raiders, combined.
The hits are distributed among them proportionately, according to each attacker's combat factor.
If the s/c faces both a bombardment and an invasion, the EWP's are fired in both cases, following the combat execution sequence detailed in section 15.9
The damage, against bombardment, is calculated as follows:

    HP=2^ET ÷ 2^MT x 0.3

Where HP is the fraction of energy units that hit, ET is the EWP's TL, MT is the MSS's TL.
In the case of an invasion, or raid, MT is the TL of their ASC or ASW.
If the attacking force is not armed with either ASC or ASW, then MT = 1.
EWP's use fuel each time they are fired.
When EWP are fired at MSS, the number of MSS destroyed = HP x the number of EWP.

#### 15.6.5.3 Energy Shields

Energy shields (ESH) are used only defensively against the beams of EWP.
ESH's operate at each attack.
Therefore, if two attackers each fired twice, the defender's ESH's would operate 4 times during that turn.
ESH's use fuel at each attack sufficient to deflect the energy beams.
One ESH can deflect 10 x the TL-squared energy units per attack, and uses 10 x TL in fuel units per attack.

##### 15.6.5.3.1 Example

Assume 3 ships (A, B, and C) attack ship D at distance 1.
A fires 2,000 EWP-1, B fires 3,000, and C fires 5,000.
D has 1,300 ESH-2.
A's 2,000 EWP-1 fire 20,000 energy units, B's fire 30,000 energy units, and C's fire 50,000 energy units.
Each ESH-2 will deflect 40 energy units per attack.
A's 20,000 energy units divided by 40 equals 500 ESH-2's used to deflect them.
The total amount of fuel used by the ESH-2's is 10,000 fuel units.
B's attack takes 750 ESH-2 to deflect it, using 15,000 fuel units.
C's attack takes 1,250 ESH-2 to deflect it, using 25,000 fuel units.
None of the attack's penetrated D's ESH's.

### 15.6.6 BOMBARDMENT MISCELLANEOUS

#### 15.6.6.1 Distances and Speed

The distance between colonies during bombardment is calculated as follows:

| From | To  | Distance |
|------|-----|----------|
| ESC  | ESC | 1        |
| ESC  | OPC | 1        |
| OPC  | ESC | 1        |
| OPC  | OPC | 1        |
| ORB  | ESC | 2        |
| ORB  | OPC | 2        |
| ORB  | ORB | 3        |

surface colony (ESC or OPC) to surface colony is 1 distance unit;
orbiting colony to surface colony is 2 distance units;
orbiting colony vs orbiting colony is 3

When a ship is involved, the ship's position is shown on the probe report section of the printout.

All colonies are assumed to be at a position of 0/0/0.
Position coordinates can not be greater than 999 or -999.
When determining MSS and EWP hits on colonies, a colony's speed is always 0.

#### 15.6.6.2 Commitment Percentage

When s/c's with multiple-TL EWP's and MSS's are involved in bombardment, the percentage commitment (percent of weapons committed) applies equally to each MSL and EWP the s/c has, regardless of the TL of each.

#### 15.6.6.3 Weighted Average

The TL's used in computing ANM and EWP hits on MSS's and troops is the *weighted average* of the TL's of the units involved.

#### 15.6.6.4 Sample Bombardment (DELETED)

    Deleted.

#### 15.6.6.5 More Miscellaneous

* ESH's, MSL's, and EWP's (used defensively) never use more than enough fuel (and in the case of MSL's, anti-missiles), to stop an attack.
  For instance, just enough ANM are used to stop incoming MSS's even if the s/c has more MSL's and ANM available.
* If the target s/c has multiple-TL ESH's, the highest TL ESH will be used first.
* Ships always take 10 times the mass units in damage that a colony does.
  For instance, a colony hit with one MSS-1 loses 100 M.U.: a ship hit with one MSS-1 loses 1,000 M.U.
  (Ships are more compact than colonies).
* A ship may not fire an EWP or MSL unless it has at least one assembled sensor on board.
* A colony can't fire on a ship unless it has an assembled SEN.
* A colony can fire on another colony without a SEN.
* Damage from bombardment against untargeted colonies and against ships is allocated randomly.
* STUN, LTSU,and SLSU all absorb damage at the same rate.
  (500 M.U. damaged will destroy 1,000 STUN or 1,000 LTSU or 1,000 SLSU).
  *On ships, the number destroyed will be 10,000 instead.*
* Ship maneuvers during combat take place on a 3-D grid, with the planet at 0/0/0.
* The scale is 1 distance unit = 10,000 km's.
* Troops and weapons are not as likely to be hit as other items are, even if they are in the designated target group.
* Ships do not collide.

## 15.7 COMBAT ORDERS ARE FORBIDDEN ON THE TURN 0 TURNSHEET

## 15.8 MILITARY SUPPLIES

### 15.8.1 WHAT THEY ARE AND WHO RECEIVES THEM

Military supplies (MTSP) consist of first aid supplies, ammunition, small arms, and other supplies.
MTSP are provided to all SLD and MRB in combat by the computer automatically; they are never included in a combat order.
The standard ration of MTSP to SLD or MRB is one MTSP each.

### 15.8.2 EFFECTS OF A SHORTAGE OF MTSP

Invaders, raiders, or supporters can not function without MTSP.
If there are no MTSP, the action will not take place; if the MTSP run out in the middle of an action, the troops will return home.
Defenders, however, can fight without MTSP, but their total combat factor is halved.

### 15.8.3 EXTRA MTSP PROVISIONS

Troops not provided with ASC or ASW are provided with 2 MTSP each, instead of one each as usual.
(This is so that they can use the small arms in the MTSP to compensate for the lack of ASC or ASW).
If there were not enough MTSP in stock for these unprovided-for troops, they would not go into battle.

## 15.9 COMBAT EXECUTION SEQUENCE

It does not matter in what order the player has his combat orders written on his turnsheet; the computer will process all orders step-by-step according to the following sequence:

### 15.9.1 PRE-MANEUVER FIRE

All EWP's and MSL's may be fired before ships maneuver.
The defensive weapons (EWP's and ANM's) defend against the energy beams and MSS's, and then the damage is calculated.

### 15.9.2 SHIP MANEUVERS

Ships will either close, stand, or run, as ordered, within the limits of the ship's speed.
A ship's speed is equal to its total thrust divided by its mass.
Speed is measured in distance units per turn.
All fractions are rounded down.
One distance unit equals 10,000 km's.
A ship may only be given one maneuver order per turn.
Total thrust is measured by adding up the thrust of all the assembled SDRV's.

### 15.9.3 POST-MANEUVER FIRE

All EWP's and MSL's may be fired after ships maneuver.
The defensive weapons defend, and the damage is calculated.

### 15.9.4 TROOP MOVEMENT

All raiding, invading, supporting, and withdrawing troops moved at this time.

### 15.9.5 DEFENSIVE WEAPONS VS TROOPS

EWP's and MSL's fire at troops, supporting troops, and raiders, (and at their ASC's and/or ASW's), that are in that s/c.

### 15.9.6 SURRENDER CHECK

If the ratio of combat factors of defenders and their supporters to raiders, or to invaders and their supporters, is 6 to 1, then the invaders surrender.
If the ratio is 1 to 6, then the defenders surrender.

### 15.9.7 INVASION CASUALTIES

Invasion casualties are computed.
The formulas for casualties are:

    AxRA=DL

and

    DxRD=AL

Where A = the attacker's combat factors; D = the defender's combat factors; RA and RD are two integers from one to five, selected randomly, divided by 10; DL = the defender's losses in combat factors, and AL = the attacker's losses in combat factors.

#### 15.9.7.1 Calculating Combat Factors

##### 15.9.7.1.1 Troops with MTSP

Each MRB counts as 2 x its TL in combat factors.
Each ASW counts as 2 x its TL squared in combat factors.
Each ASC counts as 10 x its TL in combat factors.
Each SLD counts as one combat factor.

##### 15.9.7.1.2 Troops with no MTSP

If all the troops are without MTSP, the total combat factor is cut in half.
If a portion of the troops are without MTSP, then that portion of the combat factor is cut in half.
Only *defending* troops can function without MTSP.

#### 15.9.7.2 Determining Unit Losses in Combat

The number of combat factors lost is divided by the total combat factors the side has, and the result is multiplied by the number of units (MRB, ASW, SLD or ASC) present in each force.
This result is the number of units lost.
Fractions are dropped.
10 combat factors lost ÷ 500 total combat factors equals a loss ratio of 0.02.
0.02 times a force made up of 100 SLD and 40 ASC-1 would result in 2 SLD and 0 ASC as casualties.

#### 15.9.7.3 Determining Unit Losses in Raids

Casualties from a raid are computed as above and then multiplied by afraction.
The fraction used differs according to the type of target.
The fractions are 0.3 for OPC's, 0.5 for ESC's, 0.75 for OBC's, and 0.9 for ships.

## 15.10 MISSION COMPLETION

If the defending troops have been eliminated, then the invaded s/c becomes the property of the owner of the largest invading force.
Supporters of the invader are not eligible.
65% of rebels in the captured s/c become loyal, while 65% of the loyal POP in the captured s/c become rebels; this can lead to a rebellion.
Raiders return home; raids only last one turn.
If the attacking forces have surrendered, withdrawn, or been destroyed, then the defender's supporters will WTD now.
The victor's supporters will also withdraw unless there is another invader there.
If so, the victor's supporters will support the victor against the invader.
Unfinished battles will continue on the following turn.
The victor WTD's troops on the turn following the victory.

# 17. WRITING ORDERS

## 17.1 HOW THE TURN SHEET SHOULD LOOK

The first page of the turnsheet should show the following information at the top of the page:
* the turn number (which is shown on the last printout)
* the name of the game and the game number
* the race number
* the player ID No.
* the account number (shown on the upper right hand corner of the statement)
* the player name
* the customer's signature

The orders should be neatly printed or typed.
It is recommended that a blank line be left between every three or four lines in order to reduce keypunch errors.
Orders should be written on one side of the paper only, but two or three columns per page is alright as long as the printing remains at a readable size.
The pages should be numbered if there is more than one.

## 17.2 GENERAL RULES ON WRITING ORDERS

All order lines must end in a period, and must start with a s/c ID number since all orders are addressed to s/c's.
All words in orders shown on the abbreviation list must be abbreviated.
All order lines must be less than 80 characters long, including spaces.
The s/c ID# at the beginning of the order must belong to the player writing the order.

### 17.2.1 ZEROS

Zeros do not need a slash through them.
Players may save time by eliminating the zeros from quantities in this way: 4E7 instead of 40,000,000.
The digit that follows the E represents the number of zeros to follow the digit preceding the E.
For instance, 1,000 = 1E3, 1,250,000 = 125E4, and 400,300,000 = 4003E5.
The use of E numbers is optional.
There can be no more than one E per quantity; commas and decimal points should not be used in E numbers.

### 17.2.2 COMMAS

Commas should be written in quantities where applicable (200,000), and are necessary between quantity and item, (200,000, GOLD), and are used to separate the parts of an order.
The sample turnsheet, section 17.3, has examples of this.
E numbers can not have commas in them.

### 17.2.3 TL'S

Any order dealing with TL's, (buying them, transporting them, etc.), does not use a quantity of TL.
Instead of the quantity, there is a blank space in the order.
For example:

    29, SEL, , TL-5, 50.5.

And:

    29, TFR , TL-7, 28.

### 17.2.4 RACE NUMBERS AND TL NUMBERS

All POP's, except for spies, *must* have their race number attached, as: CNW-1 or SLD-3 or PRO-2.
Spies are simply listed as SPYS.
Race numbers are NOT used in PAY orders, however.
Non-population units, (except for consumable units which have four letters each, such as CNGD, NMTS, STUN, and except for TL) *must* have their TL numbers attached.
Examples: TPT-1, SEN-4, ASC-6, AUT-8, FCT-5.

## 17.4 ORDER FORMATS AND EXAMPLES

### 17.4.1 TACTICAL MANEUVER ORDERS (TMV)

- Format: Ship ID#, "TMV", new tactical coordinates.
- Example: 250, TMV, 1,-5,2.
- Notes: The TMV order causes ship #250 to fly to position 1,-5,2 around the planet or as close to that position as the ship's speed allows.

### 17.4.2 CLOSE ORDERS (CLS)

- Format: Ship ID#, "CLS", target ship.
- Example: 250, CLS, 261.
- Notes: The CLS order moves ship 250 as close to 261 as 250's speed allows.

### 17.4.3 RUN ORDERS (RUN)

- Format: Ship ID#, "RUN", target ship.
- Example: 250, RUN, 261.
- Notes: The RUN order moves ship 250 as far away from ship 261 as 250's speed allows.

### 17.4.4 PRE-MANEUVER ENERGY WEAPON FIRE ORDERS (PME)

- Format: S/C ID#, "PME", target s/c, % of EWP firing, target.
- Example: 190, PME, 210, 10, 4.
- Notes: The PME order has s/c #190 fire 10% of its EWP at colony 210's mine groups before any ship maneuver orders are processed.

### 17.4.5 PRE-MANEUVER MISSILE FIRE ORDERS (PMM)

- Format: S/C ID#, "PMM", target s/c, % of MSL's being fired, target.
- Example: 191, PMM, 220, 30, 5.
- Notes: The PME order has colony 191 fire 30% of its MSL's at colony 220's FCT groups before any ship maneuver orders are processed.

### 17.4.6 AFTER-MANEUVER ENERGY WEAPON FIRE ORDERS (AME)

- Format: S/C ID#, "AME", target s/c, % of EWP's firing, target.
- Example: 191, AME, 220, 60.
- Notes: The AME order has s/c 191 fire 60% of its EWP's at colony 220 after all ship maneuvers are processed.
  No target was chosen (targets are optional).

### 17.4.7 AFTER-MANEUVER MISSILE FIRE ORDERS (AMM)

- Format: S/C ID#, "AMM", target s/c, % of MSL's being fired, target.
- Example: 190, AMM, 210, 25, 3.
- Notes: The AMM order has colony 190 fire 25% of its MSL's at colony 210 after all ship maneuvers are processed.

### 17.4.8 INVASION ORDERS (INV)

- Format:
  ```text
  S/C ID#, "INV", target s/c.
  Quantity, item.  (For more items, use more lines)
  1, END.  (This terminates the order and has no other significance)
  ```
- Example:
  ```text
  29, INV, 30.
  25,000, SLD-1.
  20,000, ASC-2.
  5,000, ASW-1.
  1, END.
  ```
- Notes: The INV order has s/c 29 invade s/c 30 using 25,000 SLD-1, 20,000 ASC-2, and 5,000 ASW-1.

### 17.4.9 RAID ORDERS (RAD)

- Format:
  ```text
  S/C ID#, "RAD", target s/c, item to be raided.
  Quantity, item.
  1, END.
  ```
- Example:
  ```text
  29, RAD, 200, GOLD.
  10,000, MRB-2.
  25,000, ASC-2.
  15,000, ASW-2.
  1, END.
  ```
- Notes: The RAD order has s/c 29 raid ship 200 for gold, using 10,000 MRB-2, and 25,000 ASC-2 and 15,000 ASW-2.

### 17.4.10 DEFENSIVE TROOP SUPPORT ORDERS (DTS)

- Format:
  ```text
  S/C ID#, "DTS", s/c to be supported.
  Quantity, item.
  1, END.
  ```
- Example:
  ```text
  28, DTS, 30.
  100,000, SLD-1.
  1, END.
  ```
- Notes: The DTS order has s/c 28 assign 100,000 SLD-1 to the defense of s/c 30.

### 17.4.11 OFFENSIVE TROOP SUPPORT ORDERS (OTS)

- Format:
  ```text
  S/C ID#, "OTS", target s/c, s/c to be supported
  Quantity, item.
  1, END.
  ```
- Example:
  ```text
  25, OTS, 30, 29.
  10,000, MRB-1
  10,000, ASC-2.
  10,000, ASW-1.
  1, END.
  ```
- Notes: The OTS order has s/c 25 assign 10,000 MRB-1, 10,000 ASC-2, and 10,000 ASW-1 to the support of colony 29 in its attack on s/c 30.

### 17.4.12 WITHDRAW ORDERS (WTD)

- Format:
  ```text
  S/C ID#, "WTD", target s/c.
  Quantity, item.
  1, END.
  ```
- Example:
  ```text
  27, WTD, 17.
  15,000, SLD-1.
  10, ASC-1.
  1, END.
  ```
- Notes: The WTD order has s/c 27 withdraw from s/c 17.
  WTD is an all-or-nothing order.

### 17.4.13 PERMISSION TO COLONIZE ORDERS (PTC)

- Format: Colony ID#, "PTC", ship ID requesting permission.
- Example: 308, PTC, 479.
- Notes: The PTC order, written by 308, the controller of the planet, gives permission to ship 479 to set up a colony there.

### 17.4.14 ALLOW ADD-ON ORDERS (AAN)

- Format: S/C ID#, "AAN", s/c to do the add-on.
- Example: 205, AAN, 38.
- Notes: The AAN order allows s/c 38 to add-on items to s/c 205.

#### 17.4.14.1 Multiple Target AAN Orders

- Format: S/C ID#, "AAN", s/c's to do the add-ons.
- Example: 205, AAN, 38, 39, 42, 46, 48, 49.
- Notes: The multiple target AAN order allows s/c's 38, 39, 42, 46, 48, and 49 to add-on items to s/c 205.

### 17.4.15 SET UP ORDERS (STP)

- Format:
  ```text
  S/C ID#, "STP", type of s/c to be set up.
  Quantity, item.
  Quantity to be set up, "END".
  ```
- Example:
  ```text
  29, STP, SHP.
  50,000, STUN.
  5, PRO-2.
  1, SDRV-1.
  20, FOOD.
  5, LFS-1.
  1, SEN-1.
  1000, FUEL.
  100,HDRV-1.
  20, CNGD.
  6, END.
  ```
- Notes: The STP order has colony 29 set up 6 identical ships.
  (There is never more than one colony of any type set up on any turn).
  The items were listed in the order of their importance, with the STUN heading the list.
  Only one quantity and one type of unit is allowed per line.
  Note that the 5,000 FCT-1 will go into storage, but the 6,000 FCT-1 will be assembled and begin to make TPT-1.
  In order to STP a s/c for another player, the receiving player's ID# is entered after "the type of s/c to be set up" on the first line of the order.
  A s/c can be set up for a member of the same race only.

### 17.4.16 ADD-ON ORDERS (ADN)

- Format:
  ```text
  S/C ID#, "ADN", s/c being added-on to.
  Quantity, item.
  1, END.
  ```
- Example:
  ```text
  38, ADN, 205.
  1,400,000, SLSU.
  12, LFS-1.
  10,000, FCT-1.
  6,000, FCT-1, FCT-2.
  1,000, FCT-1, 3.
  1, END.
  ```
- Notes: The ADN order has colony 38 ADN to colony 205.
  As in the STP order, the items are listed in the order of their importance, with the SLSU listed first, and only one quantity and one type of unit is allowed per line.
  Add-on orders always finish with **1, END**, never with any other quantity.
  The 10,000 FCT-1 will go into storage, the 6,000 FCT-1 will be assembled and begin manufacturing FCT-2, and the 1,000 FCT-1 will be added to FCT group #3 already in colony 205.

#### 17.4.16.1 Multiple Target ADN Orders

- Format:
  ```text
  S/C ID#, "ADN", s/c's being added-on to.
  Quantity, item.
  1, END.
  ```
- Example:
  ```text
  38, ADN, 205, 206, 207, 208, 209, 210.
  1,400,000, SLSU.
  12, LFS-1.
  300, PRO-2.
  1, END.
  ```
- Notes: The multiple target ADN order allows the listed items to be added-on to ships 205, 206, 207, 208, 209, and 210.

### 17.4.17 JUNK ORDERS (JNK)

- Format: S/C ID#, "JNK", s/c to be junked.
- Example: 698, JNK, 299.
- Notes: The JNK order causes s/c 299 to be dis-assembled into its component parts, which are placed in storage in s/c 698.
  Only s/c's may be junked.

#### 17.4.17.1 Multiple Target JNK Orders

- Format: S/C ID#, "JNK", s/c's to be junked.
- Example: 698, JNK, 299, 300, 301, 302.
- Notes: The multiple target junk order causes s/c's 299, 300, 301, and 302, to be dis-assembled, and their component parts to be placed into 698's storage.

### 17.4.18 GIVE ORDERS (GIV)

- Format: S/C being given, "GIV", *player ID#* receiving.
- Example: 37, GIV, 54.
- Notes: The GIV order causes colony 37 to be given to player 54.
  This applies only to s/c's.
  An s/c can't be given to a foreign player.

### 17.4.19 ACCEPT ORDERS (ACP)

- Format: S/C being received, ACP.
- Example: 37, ACP.
- Notes: The ACP order causes s/c 37 to be received by the player writing the order (he does not write his own player number); he takes control of the s/c.

### 17.4.20 MERGE ORDERS (MRG)

- Format: S/C ID#, "MRG", s/c that is being absorbed.
- Example: 45, MRG, 50.
- Notes: The MRG order causes s/c 50 to merge into s/c 45 and be absorbed.
  After the merge, 45 exists, and 50 does not.
  Both s/c's must be owned by the same player, the total number of FCT groups in both s/c's must total less than 25, and both s/c's must be of the same type.

### 17.4.21 COMBINE ORDERS (COM)

- Format: S/C ID#, "COM", FCT group, FCT group that is being absorbed.
- Example: 77, COM, 1, 7.
- Notes: The COM order causes FCT group 7 to combine with FCT group 1, and be absorbed.
  After they combine, FCT group 1 exists, and FCT group 7 does not.
  Both FCT groups must be making the same kind of unit, and both must have the same type of work in process.

### 17.4.22 DEPOSIT ORDERS (DEP)

- Format: S/C ID#, "DEP", receiving s/c, mine group #.
- Example: 84, DEP, 76, 5.
- Notes: The DEP order has s/c 84 give mine group 5, including the deposit it is on, to s/c 76.

### 17.4.23 SCRAP ORDERS (SCR)

- Format: S/C ID#, "SCR", quantity, unit to be scrapped.
- Example: 55, SCR, 1000, ASW-1.
- Notes: The SCR order has s/c 55 convert the ASW's to resource units.
  POP's can not be scrapped.

#### 17.4.23.1 Multiple Scrap Orders

- Format:
  ```text
  S/C ID#, "SCR", quantity of unit types, M.
  quantity, unit.
  ```
- Example:
  ```text
  38, SCR, 5, M.
  20,000, CNGD.
  1,000, ASW-1.
  562, ASW-2.
  900, MSS-1.
  100, MTSP.
  ```

The multiple SCR is used to scrap more than one item per s/c per turn.
This is optional; the player may write each order separately if he wishes.
This order does not require "END" to finish it.

### 17.4.24 TRANSFER ORDERS (TFR)

- Format: S/C ID#, "TFR", quantity, item, receiving s/c.
- Example: 75, TFR, 1,000,000, NMTS, 74.
- Notes: The TFR order has s/c 75 transfer 1,000,000 NMTS TO s/c 74.
  The items are taken from 75's storage and are put into 74's storage.
  Any item can be TFR'd to another Ruler of the same race if the receiving s/c writes a REC order.
  Only gold can be TFR'd to a Ruler of another race.
  Group G SPYS may be TFR'd into a foreign s/c to conduct espionage, but they remain under their Ruler's control (see 11.1).

#### 17.4.24.1 Multiple Target TFR Orders

- Format: S/C ID#, "TFR", quantity, item, receiving s/c's.
- Example: 75, TFR, 30,000, RSCH, 70, 71, 72, 73, 74.
- Notes: The multiple target TFR order causes the RSCH to be transferred to 70, 71, 72, 73, and 74.

#### 17.4.24.2 Multiple TFR Orders

- Format: S/C ID#, "TFR", quantity of unit types, M, receiving s/c (or s/c's).
- Example:
  ```text
  75, TFR, 3, M, 307, 308, 310, 311.
  100, CNGD.
  5,000, FOOD.
  30,000, RSCH.
  ```
- Notes: The multiple TFR order is optional.
  It can be used to TFR more than one item to one or more s/c's.
  In this case, it TFR'd three items to each of four s/c's.

#### 17.4.24.3 TFR's of Population

Rebels can be moved along with other population units in a TFR only.
They will never be moved in pick-ups, add-ons, or set ups.
To prevent the rebels from being transferred, change the last letter of the POP abbreviation to X.
For instance, 54, TFR, 500, PRX-3, 398.
This TFR order TFR's 500 PRO-3 to s/c 398 without rebels.

    Note: POP can not be TFR's to s/c 0 from a home colony.

### 17.4.25 RECEIVE ORDERS (REC)

- Format: S/C ID#, "REC", quantity, item, s/c received from.
- Example: 307, REC, 1E6, NMTS, 75.
- Notes: The REC order has s/c 307 receive 1,000,000 NMTS from s/c #75.
  This order is not necessary if both s/c's are owned by the same player.
  If X- POP units are used in a REC order, it will be void.
  REC orders are used only for TFR's from another Ruler of the same race, or his Regent (see 11.1).
  Gold, RSCH, and SPYS do not require a REC order.

#### 17.4.25.1 Multiple Target REC Orders

- Format: S/C ID#, "REC", quantity, item, s/c's received from.
- Example: 75, REC, 3E3, NMTS, 72, 73.
- Notes: The multiple target REC order causes the NMTS to be received from 72 and 73.

#### 17.4.25.2 To Cancel Receive Orders

REC orders remain in force until used or canceled.
To do this, the order should be written as before, except that the qty # being REC'd is changed to 0, as in 29, REC, 0, FOOD, 25.

#### 17.4.25.3 Multiple REC Orders

- Format: S/C ID#, "REC", quantity of unit types, M, s/c (or s/c's) received from.
- Example:
  ```text
  307, REC, 4, M, 75, 76, 77, 78, 79.
  100, PRO-4.
  200, LFS-1.
  10, TPT-2.
  25, AUT-1.
  ```
- Notes: The multiple REC order is optional.
  It can be used to REC more than one item from one or more s/c's.
  In this case, it received 4 items from each of five s/c's.
  If the player wishes, he can write each REC order separately.

### 17.4.26 PICK UP ORDERS (PUP)

- Format: S/C ID#, "PUP", quantity, item, s/c being picked up from.
- Example: 69, PUP, 10, SDRV-1, 70.
- Notes: The PUP order has s/c 69 pick-up SDRV-1 from s/c 70.
  X-POP units may not be used in a pick up order; is used, it will be void.
  The items are picked up from 70's storage and go into 69's storage.
  Any item can be PUP'd from another Ruler of the same race if that s/c writes an APU order.
  Nothing can be PUP'd from a Ruler of another race, except the player's own group G SPYS (see 11.1).

#### 17.4.26.1 Multiple Target PUP Orders

- Format: S/C ID#, "PUP", quantity, item, s/c's being picked up from.
- Example: 307, PUP, 100, HDRV-1, 75, 74, 72.
- Notes: The multiple target PUP order causes the HDRV-1 to be picked up from 75, 74, and 72.

#### 17.4.26.2 Multiple PUP Orders

- Format: S/C ID#, "PUP", quantity of unit types, M, s/c (or s/c's) to be PUP'd from.
- Example:
  ```text
  307, PUP, 3, M, 80, 81.
  2E6, LTSU.
  800, FOOD.
  90, CNW-2.
  ```
- Notes: The multiple PUP order is optional.
  It can be used to PUP more than one item from one or more s/c's.
  In the case, it picked up 3 items from each of two s/c's.
  If the player wishes, he can write each PUP order separately.

### 17.4.27 ALLOW PICK UP ORDERS (APU)

- Format: S/C ID#, "APU", quantity, item, s/c making pick up.
- Example: 70, APU, 10, SDRV-1, 69.
- Notes: The APU order has s/c 70 allow s/c 69 to pick up 70's 10 SDRV-1.
  X-POP can not be used in an APU order or it will be void.
  APU orders are not used if both s/c's are owned by the same player.
  APU orders are used only for PUP's by another Ruler of the same race, or his Regent (see 11.1).

#### 17.4.27.1 Multiple Target APU Orders

- Format: S/C ID#, "APU", quantity, item, s/c's making pick up.
- Example: 75, APU, 1, GOLD, 72, 73, 74.
- Notes: The multiple target APU order has s/c 75 allow the three s/c's to pick up 1 gold unit each from s/c 75.

##### 17.4.27.1.1 Cancelling APU orders

APU orders remain in force until either used or cancelled.
In order to cancel an APU order, the player writes it as before, substituting 0 for the quantity.

- Example: 38, APU, 0, GOLD, 292.

#### 17.4.27.2 Multiple APU orders

- Format: S/C ID#, "APU", quantity of unit types, M, s/c (s), making pick up.
- Example:
  ```text
  75, APU, 2, M, 66, 67, 68, 69, 70, 71, 72, 73, 74.
  300,500, SLSU.
  400, PRO-5.
  ```
- Notes: The multiple APU order is optional.
  It can be used to have more than one item picked up by one or more s/c's.
  In this case, it has s/c 75 allow 9 s/c's to each pick up 300,300 SLSU and 400 PRO-5 from s/c 75.
  If the player wishes, he can write each APU order separately.

### 17.4.28 BUILD CHANGE ORDERS (BCH)

- Notes: This BCH order has FCT group 8 begin making RSCH.

- Example: 16, BCH, 8, SHT.

### 17.4.37 SPY MOVE ORDERS (SPM)

- Format: S/C where spies are, "SPM", quantity, from group #, to group #.
- Example: 28, SPM, 20, G, E.
- Notes: SPYS cannot be moved from one s/c to another with a SPM order; they are moved with a TFR order.
  In order to be TFR'd, they must be in group G.
  On arrival in the new s/c, they are still in group G, and must be changed to another group to be effective.
  This order changes the SPYS from group G to E.

- Example: 18, SPM, 29, E, C.
- Notes: The spies were changed from group E to C.

- Example: 18, SPM, 45, C, G.
- Notes: The spies were changed to group G.
  They must be changed to group G before they can go home.
  In order to go home, they are picked up (not TFRd).
  To send them from one foreign s/c to another, they must be picked up and returned home.
  They then can be TFR'd to another s/c.

- Format: S/C where spies are, "SPM", quantity, from grp, to grp, target 1, target 2.
- Example: 29, SPM, 10, G, F, 5.
- Notes: Targets are optional, and are only used for group F and group D spies.
  In this case, 10 spies in group G are moved to group F with a target of 5.

- Example: 29, SPM, 0, F, F, 5.
- Notes: 0 used as a quantity in orders for group F and group D spies always signifies "all".
  Therefore, all the spies in group F (with no target) are assigned to group F *target 5*.

- Example: 29, SPM, 10, G, F, 0.
- Notes: No target was specified, so the information reported by the F SPYS will be randomly selected by the computer.

- Format: S/C where spies are, "SPM", from group #, to group #, target 1, target 2.
- Example: 29, SPM, 1000, G, D, 0, 0.
- Notes: No target was specified for these D spies, so they will kill all SPYS except those of their Ruler of any type belonging to anyone.

- Example: 29, SPM, 1000, C, D, 0, A.
- Notes: 0 used as a target means all, so the 1000 spies have been changed from C spies to D spies, and instructed to kill all A spies.
  The first target for D SPYS signifies whose spies they are to assassinate, and the second target signifies the type of spies they are to kill.

- Example: 29, SPM, 1000, C, D, 26, 0.
- Notes: This format changes 1000 spies from C to D, ordering them to kill all SPYS belonging to player 26.

- Example: 29, SPM, 1000, A, D, 28, B.
  This SPM order changes 1000 spies from group A to group D, and orders them to kill all B spies belonging to player 28.

- Example: 29, SPM, 0, D, D, 27, 0.
- Notes: This SPM order has *all* the D spies now ordered to assassinate all spies belonging to 27.

NOTE: ALL SPM ORDERS CAN BE USED IN THE PLAYER'S OWN S/C, OR IN FOREIGN S/C's.

# 18. REBELLION

Many of the rules listed below are approximations, meant to serve as a guide.
It is hard for a leader to know what is in the hearts of his subjects, so there is an element of unpredictability regarding rebels.
Rebels are the units in each population group (except spies and soldiers) who are willing to rebel.
Rebel soldiers are soldier units who are willing to rebel.
Spies do not rebel.

## 18.1 INCREASES

### 18.1.1 REBEL INCREASE CHART

| CAUSE OF INCREASE                                               | APPROXIMATE AMOUNT OF INCREASE                                  |
|-----------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------|
| Capture by a foreign power                                      | 65% of loyal POP's become rebels.                               |
| Standard of living: 0.5 to 1 \*\*                               | 1 to 5% of current rebel pop.                                   |
| Standard of living: 0.1 to 0.5 \*\*                             | 10-50% of current rebel pop.                                    |
| Standard of living: below 0.1 \*\*                              | 50% or more of current rebel pop.                               |
| Food rationing                                                  | 1-75% of current rebel pop.                                     |
| Starvation \*\*                                                 | 75% or more of current rebel pop.                               |
| Lack of housing (in OPC's only)                                 | 25% of unhoused pop become rebels.                              |
| Jettisoning POP's.                                              | 10 x number jettisoned become rebels.                           |
| POP's become under the control of a player of a different race. | 1% to 10% of those POP's become rebels                          |
| Back pay                                                        | The ratio of back pay to POP's determines the rate of increase. |
| Paying one POP group much lower or higher than other groups     | 0 to 1% of current total population.                            |

\*\* This cause has rebels increase, not only from the rebel population as shown, but also by one to ten percent of the total population.

### 18.1.2 REBEL SOLDIER INCREASES

Rebel soldiers increase due to disproportionate pay, at a rate of usually no more than 2% of the current total population.
Rebel soldiers increase in proportion to the number of rebels each turn.
The larger the quantity of rebels, the more rebel soldiers will be "recruited" each turn from the loyal SLD units.

## 18.2 DECREASES

If a s/c is captured by a foreign power, 65% of the rebels in that s/c become loyal.
Rebels and rebel soldiers also decrease when the standard of living rises, and when they are converted by group B SPYS back into loyal POP units.
A s/c's group B spies converts 1.5 rebels or rebel soldiers per turn.
Visiting group B spies convert only one rebel or rebel soldier per turn.
The group B spies convert the rebel soldiers first, and when done, convert rebels.

## 18.3 REBELLION

10% of all rebels are considered activists and will fight if a rebellion takes place.
All rebel soldiers will fight if a rebellion takes place.
A rebellion will take place if the combined total of rebels and rebel soldiers is equal to 60% of the total population of the s/c.
Rebel soldiers will attempt to steal ASC and ASW for the rebellion.
The number stolen is roughly proportional to the percentage of soldiers that are rebel soldiers.
The rebellion proceeds in combat, with the activists and rebel soldiers treated as invaders, and loyal soldiers and MRB's defending.

## 18.4 AFTERMATH

If the rebels and rebel soldiers lose, then 70% of their survivors become loyal.
All losing SLD and rebel soldier survivors become UEM's.
If the rebels and rebel soldiers win, then the number of rebels and rebel soldiers is reduced to 0, as they are now considered to be loyal to the new regime.
The s/c then is given to the Ruler with the most group E spies in the colony.
If that Ruler or one of his Regents was the former owner, then the Ruler with the second highest number of E spies gets control.
If the Ruler with the largest number of group E spies (not counting any group E spies of the former owner) has exactly the same amount of E spies in the s/c as another Ruler, then the s/c is given to an independent player.
The s/c is also given to an independent if the s/c has no group E spies other than the former owner's.

## 18.5 SUPPORT DURING REBELLION

Since rebellions are treated as invasions, other s/c's may support the loyalist population by writing DTS orders supporting the s/c that is having the rebellion.
Or, they may support the rebels by writing OTS orders supporting s/c #0.
Suppose, for instance, s/c 99 is having a rebellion.
If player 95 wants to support s/c 99, he would write: 95, DTS, 99.
If he wants to support the rebels in 99, he would write: 95, OTS, 99, 0.

# 19. ESPIONAGE

## 19.1 GENERAL RULES

Espionage is performed by spy units (SPYS) and can be conducted in either ships or colonies.
Each spy unit is composed of one SLD and one PRO.
There can be only one type of espionage activity performed by a spy unit per turn, and there can be no more than one of each type of spy group per s/c per turn.
(Rulers can not assign the same 100 SPYS to be in both group B and group E on the same turn, and they cannot assign two group D spy groups to the same s/c.)
SPYS may not be sold as spies, but may be dis-assembled into PRO's and SLD's and then sold.
SPYS may be killed, but they will never desert, rebel, or surrender.
SPYS use up food, CNGD's, and life support, whether in the home s/c or in a foreign s/c.
(SPYS in a foreign s/c steal their food and pay from the host's s/c.
They do this by forging ration tickets and vouchers.
The host's death, pay, and ration rates apply).

## 19.2 GROUP G SPYS

To form a spy group, the player writes an ASM order.
When a spy group is assembled, it automatically becomes group G spy group.
Group G SPYS may be viewed as a pool from which one draws spies for any other group; they perform no espionage activity in themselves.
Only group G spies, however, can be transferred to, or picked up from, another s/c.
In order for SPYS to function, they must be physically at the s/c in which they are to work.
To get SPYS to a foreign s/c, one must transfer them there with a TFR order.
(If the SPYS are not group G spies, the TFR will not work; they must be changed from the group they are in to group G with a SPM order).
SPYS being TFR'd to another s/c will not enter if the structural limitations would be exceeded.
SPYS are still controlled by their Ruler regardless of the s/c they are in.

SPYS being TFR'd leave their TPT's just outside the s/c.
The SPYS will be shot at but not their TPT's, unless the owner of the s/c is their Ruler

### 19.2.1 CASUALTIES

The following formula determines the percentage of SPYS caught and shot when entering and leaving a foreign s/c.

| S/C Type | Percent Caught |
|----------|----------------|
| ESC      | 50%            |
| OBC      | 50%            |
| OPC      | 10%            |
| SHP      | 50%            |

Casualties less than one will be rounded up to one.

## GROUP G SPYS, CONT.

On the turn following the TFR of the group G SPYS, the player may assign the G SPYS to various functions.
He does this by assigning all or part of the G SPYS to various spy groups, each of which has a separate function, using SPM orders.

(Of course, a Ruler may use some SPYS within his home colony.
These SPYS he would not TFR, but simply assign to the appropriate spy group).
When the SPYS that are in the foreign s/c have completed their task, and the Ruler is ready to bring them home, he moves them back into group G (with a SPM order), and then issues a pick-up order.
(SPYS cannot be TFR'd from a s/c that does not belong to their Ruler.
Nor can they be picked up by a s/c that does not belong to their Ruler).
The SPYS leave the s/c, being shot at, (with the same rules and casualty percentages that apply to entering a foreign s/c), and meet their TPT's outside the s/c.
The TPT's then bring the SPYS to their colony.
The SPYS may not be moved from one foreign s/c to another foreign s/c directly.

Espionage activities are separated into various functions, which are performed by a particular group.
Assigning SPYS is done with a SPM order; which causes them to start functioning immediately.
SPYS in any group may be moved with a SPM order into any other group, as long as both groups are in the same s/c.

## 19.3 GROUP A SPYS

These SPYS are counter-intelligence spies and are usually used in their Ruler's own s/c's.
They inform the player of the number of rebels and rebel soldiers in his s/c's.
Although only one group A spy unit is necessary per s/c to give this information, it is a good idea to have more in case some are killed.
Group A spies are also used in enemy s/c's, for intelligence.
A knowledge of the rebel condition in the enemy's s/c's can be very valuable.

## 19.4 GROUP B SPYS

These SPYS convert rebels and rebel soldiers back into loyal population units.
Each group B spy unit can convert (rebel soldiers first, then rebels when all the rebel soldiers have been converted) 1.5 per turn in a s/c owned by their Ruler or his Regents; in foreign s/c's, they convert 1 per turn.

## 19.5 GROUP C SPYS

These SPYS will report to their Ruler the number of other spies in the ship or colony (either foreign or domestic) where they are located.
Their report will designate these foreign spies by group number and the number of the s/c from which they originated.
Only one spy unit per s/c is required for this function but more may be necessary in case of assassinations.

## 19.6 GROUP D SPYS

These SPYS are used to assassinate other spies.
They may be used in either domestic or foreign s/c's.
Group D spies have targets; they can be told which group of SPYS to kill and/or which Ruler's spies are to be killed.
Examples of this are shown in the SPM orders in Chapter 17, Writing Orders.

### 19.6.1 ASSASSINATION CASUALTIES

Attacking group D spies will destroy a number of defending spies equal to one third of the attacker's number.
The number of attacking spies lost is equal to one half of the defenders killed.
For example: if 21 SPYS attacked 12 SPYS, 3 attacking SPYS and 7 defending SPYS would be lost.
Fractions are rounded down.
Domestic group D spies always strike first.

## 19.7 GROUP E SPYS

These SPYS incite rebellion, and are always used in foreign s/c's.
Each group E spy unit can convert one loyal population unit to one rebel unit.
More about this is covered in Chapter 18, Rebellion.

## 19.8 GROUP F SPYS

Group F SPYS can obtain for their Ruler a part of a foreign s/c's report.
Reminder: the F spies have to be *at* that s/c to get that information.
Whether the spies *can* obtain any portion of a report is determined by the following formula:

| PERCENTAGE OF FOREIGN POP COMPOSED OF F SPYS | CHANCE OF SUCCESS IN OBTAINING DATA |
|----------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------|
| 0.00001% to 0.00009%                         | 10%                                 |
| 0.0001% to 0.00049%                          | 20%                                 |
| 0.0005% to 0.00099%                          | 30%                                 |
| 0.001% to 0.00499%                           | 40%                                 |
| 0.005% to 0.00999%                           | 50%                                 |
| 0.01% to 0.04999%                            | 60%                                 |
| 0.05% to 0.09999%                            | 70%                                 |
| 0.1% to 0.49999%                             | 80%                                 |
| 0.5% plus                                    | 90%                                 |

If the group F spies can obtain a section of the report, the section they receive is selected randomly, unless a specific section is requested.

### 19.8.1 SECTIONS OF GROUP F REPORTS

Each report has 6 sections that can be retrieved by group F spies.
Each section is identified by its own target number, as shown below:

| Target Number | Section                            |
|---------------|------------------------------------|
| 1             | Census or personnel report         |
| 2             | Unassembled or non-assembly items. |
| 3             | Assembled items                    |
| 4             | Mining report                      |
| 5             | Manufacturing report               |
| 6             | Transfer and market report         |

Using the random selection, there is a 16.67% chance of getting any section.
If the group F SPYS are assigned to a particular target (section of the report), then the chances of getting that particular section are increased to 33.4% and decreased to 13.33% for the other sections.

## 19.9 REGENTS

Regents may assemble spies, but may not issue SPM orders.
All spies assembled by a Regent are controlled by his Ruler.
A Regent can dis-assemble any group G spies in his s/c's.
A Regent can also PUP and TFR spies.
This will be shown on the printout under pickups and transfers, but not in the spy reports.

# 20. COMMUNICATIONS

There are no prohibitions regarding communications; players may speak to whom they wish.
The players may communicate through mail or phone, or via News Release and Diplomatic Message, as shown below.

## 20.1 NEWS SERVICE

The News Service operates on a race wide basis.
It contains the Market Report or Reports, the Combat Report, and News Releases from the members of the race.
The News Service is part of each printout, and is given to all members of each race, including Regents and Independents.

### 20.1.1 NEWS RELEASES

A News Release is a message from a member of a race to the other members of the race.
It must be oriented to the game.
Total news releases per player per turn are limited to 10 lines of 60 characters each, including blanks and punctuation.
News releases over this limit will cost more.
Offensive language is edited.
News releases may contain the player's real name and address, they may be anonymous, or lies.

## 20.2 DIPLOMATIC MESSAGES

Diplomatic messages should pertain to the game and may be as long as desired, and are sent in with a player's turn.
They must be written on separate pieces of paper (any larger than 2" x 2" will do) for each addressee, and must show the game, game number, and the addressee's ID#.
Diplomatic messages may not be signed with a false name.
They may be addressed to members of the same race, or to s/c's located at the same location as one of the sender's s/c's.

# 21. AUXILLARY PLAYERS

## 21.1 REGENTS

Regents (formerly called Subordinates) are players who run one or more s/c's for a single Ruler.
Any number of s/c's may be managed by a single Regent.
A Ruler may advise his Regent and issue commands to him, but the only actual control he has over him is to remove him from the game.
The Ruler removes him by sending a postcard or note to the Moderator, telling him the name of the Regent and the ID numbers of the s/c's he manages.
A Ruler acquires a Regent by sending a request for one to the Moderator.
The moderator assigns a player number to the Regent and puts him in touch with his Ruler, who gives him s/c's with a GIV order.
(The regent accepts them with an ACP order).
When a Regent is removed, the s/c is gifted back to the Ruler on the turn we are notified. \*\*
A Regent may use any of the orders listed in Chapter 17, with the following exceptions: A Regent cannot fulfill the victory conditions.
Any planets he controls are counted towards his Ruler's total.
A Regent, however, may be part of a winning race, in a racial victory.
Regents are allowed to use GIV and ACP orders only with their own Rulers, or with other Regents of the same Ruler.
A Regent can not use SPM orders.
Any spies he assembles are controlled by his Ruler.

\*\* The s/c is gifted back to the Ruler during the disassembly stage, (16.2.6.5).

## 21.2 INDEPENDENTS

Independents are Rulers who have been given control of a s/c that has had a successful rebellion.
All rules, including victory conditions, apply to Independents.
If the population of a newly rebelled s/c is composed of mixed races, then the race number of the new Independent will be the same as that of the majority of the population.
Planets controlled by Independents count towards their race's victory total, as well as towards the Independent's total.

# 22. VICTORY CONDITIONS

## 22.1 INDIVIDUAL VICTORY

When a single Ruler gains control of 15% or more of the total habitability, (the sum of all the habitability numbers of all the planets in the game), for 4 consecutive turns and no other single Ruler has half as much, the game is over and that Ruler wins.

## 22.2 RACIAL VICTORY

When a race, as a group, controls 50% or more of the total habitability in the game for four consecutive turns while no other race controls half as much, then a racial victory is declared.

# APPENDIX I: MAXI-CHALLENGE

MAXI-CHALLENGE differs from EMPYREAN CHALLENGE in the following ways:

1. Each Ruler starts with the resources of an entire planet.
   The units a Ruler starts the game with are listed on the pre-game turnsheet.
2. The News Service does not exist.
3. There are 20 players per game, instead of 150.
4. The market does not exist.
5. Gold does not exist in MAXI-CHALLENGE, as there will probably be little trade between players.
6. The efficiency factor used here is always **1** when calculating cost, and is never less than **1** when calculating personnel requirements.
   (See 8.3, Manufacturing).
7. TFR, APU, REC, ADN, and STP orders are handled as in the standard rules (see 7.4.3.5, 7.5.8, and 11.1).
8. Home planets can be controlled exactly like non-home planets
9. The victory conditions are:
   - A. If all the existing colonies (not ships) belong to one Ruler and his Regents, they win and the game is over.
   - B. A Ruler and his Regents can win by scoring 15,000 victory points.
     No Victory points can be scored on turn 0.
     Fractions of points are dropped.
     Losing control of a s/c results in a loss of 20 victory points.
     The victory points consist of colony points, ship points, and planet points.
     - a. Colony points are counted as follows:
       - aa. (Colony POP x standard of living) divided by 10,000,000.
         If the standard of living is higher than 2, the computer uses 2 for the SOL figure.
       - bb. (FCT's that are assembled and not building RSCH or retooling x the FCT's TL) / by 1 million.
     - b. Ship points are counted as follows:
       - aa. (Assembled HDRV's x each HDRV's TL) divided by 10,000 OR (SHP's mass divided by 0.6 ) divided by 10,000,000; whichever is LESS.
         Note: SHP's mass does not include the mass of FOOD.
     - C. Planet points are counted as follows:
       - aa. Habitability number of controlled planet times 3.
       - bb. Population on a planet is counted in OPC's only.
       - cc. Habitability points are only awarded where the POP is 200,000 per habitability point.
         I.E., a hab 25 planet requires a POP of 5E6 in order to get the FULL 75 victory points, (five million POP divided by 2E5 POP per hab point = 25. 25 x 3 = 75 victory points).
         A hab 25 planet requires a POP of at least 2E5 to get 3 victory points.*
       - dd. For MAXI-CHALLENGE bitterend games, planet points are counted the same as in EMPYREAN CHALLENGE.

\* Thus, planet points are awarded for the number of population units you control on a given habitable planet in OPC's rather than for simply controlling the planet.
In theory, players could share a planet.
Oldest OPC will have first claim on hab points on any given planet.

10. MAXI-CHALLENGE HEAD START:
    Head start games are versions of MAXI-CHALLENGE.
    All the MAXI-CHALLENGE rules apply except for:
    - A. Each player starts with TL-5.
    - B. All starting materials are TL-5 items except for FRMs (-1).
    - C. Tech levels cost ten times as much in research as they do in other MC games.
      That is, TL-6 will cost 16 million research points, and TL-7 will cost 32 million.
    - D. Points are counted as in MC, but they are not cumulative.
    - E. The winner is the first player to create a 2500 point empire.
      If two or more achieve this on the same turn, then the highest point count wins.
      Also, if the number of rulers drops below 5, the game ends and the highest point count wins.
11. MAXI-CHALLENGE BITTER END:
    Bitterend uses the same rules as MAXI-CHALLENGE with the following exceptions:
    - A. The cost of tech-levels is 10 times a normal MC game, (same as in Head Start).
    - B. All 40 deposits at the home planet will be full at the start of the game.
    - C. The winner is the first ruler to control 50% or more of the total habitability in the game for four consecutive turns, during which no other player can control half as much habitability.

# APPENDIX II: THE TEN PLANET WAR

THE TEN PLANET WAR is a variation of EMPYREAN CHALLENGE.
All EMPYREAN CHALLENGE rules apply to THE TEN PLANET WAR except for the following:

1. The News Service is deleted.
2. The Market is deleted.
3. There are no Regents or Independents in this game.
4. This game is played in only one solar system, which has 10 planets.
5. Each player starts on one of the planets.
6. There are only ten players per game.
7. The player who has s/c's remaining after all other players' have been eliminated wins the game.
8. A player is eliminated when all of his s/c's have been captured or destroyed and when all of his spies have been killed.
9. The units a player starts off with are detailed on the Ten Planet War pre-game turn sheet.
10. The efficiency factor (see 8.3, Manufacturing) is always 1 when calculating cost, and is never less than 1 when calculating personnel requirements.
11. TFR, APU, REC, ADN, and STP orders are handled as in the standard rules (see 7.4.3.5, 7.5.8, and 11.1).
12. Farms may be placed in orbiting colonies, but not in ships.
13. Farms and factories in orbiting colonies use no fuel.

# PRINT OUT INTERPRETATION

Most of the printout is in easy to understand terms, with the exceptions of the abbreviations listed in the Glossary, the error messages, orbit probes, and the three headings shown below.

1. Space Available is the number of additional volume units a s/c can hold.
2. Transport Not Used is the total unused carrying capacity of all of the TPT's in the s/c measured in mass units.
3. Standard of Living is the total CNGD's paid divided by the total population and multiplied by 4 (which makes the rate annual).
4. *Orbit Probes:*

```text
                                                          tactical
Format:   S/C type  S/C name (S/C ID#)  mass=log base 10  coordinates
Example:  SHP       VEGA-5   (356)      M=6               9/-28/13
          OPC       BADSHOT  (28)       M=8               0/0/0
```

# ERROR MESSAGES

An error message is the error code at the end of an order that failed.
The failed orders are separated into two headings on the printout.
Most orders that fail are shown under the heading "Special Messages".
Each s/c a player has gets its own report on the printout.
Special Messages are messages from that particular s/c to the player.
Since most orders are for a s/c owned by the player, most orders that fail would be shown under Special Messages.
Some orders are for a foreign s/c, like SPM orders.
These orders that fail show up under the heading, "Player's Special Messages".
The error code at the end of a failed order explains why the order failed.

- E01: Format error.
  This error also includes mis-spellings and undefined errors.
- E02: Quantity error.
  E02 may mean lack of fuel for TPT's involved, but in most cases refers to the quantity of the object of the order.
- E03: The player does not own the s/c or planet necessary.
- E04: The s/c does not exist.
- E05: E5 error codes have been discontinued.
- E06: The deposit is already being mined.
- E07: The FCT or MIN group does not exist.
- E08: The player already has 40 MIN groups, and can not have more.
- E09: The FCT group cannot make the new item until the FCT's are retooled.
- E10: There is no market at location of the s/c.
- E11: There is not enough space available in the s/c to complete the order.
- E12: The player already has 25 FCT groups, and can not have more.
- E13: The s/c was at the wrong orbit.
- E14: No REC, AAN, or APU order was given.
- E15: Insufficient TPT capacity or CNW's.
- E16: Permission to colonize was not granted.
- E17: A REC, AAN, or APU order was written, but for a lesser amount.
- E18: The planet is already controlled.
- E19: There is no planet in that orbit.
